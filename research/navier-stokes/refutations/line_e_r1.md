# Refutation report — Line E (logarithmically supercritical hyperdissipation), round 1

Referee stance: hostile, default-to-refuted. Files reviewed: `line_e_frontier.md` and `line_e_frontier.py`, against `00_problem.md` sections 0, 2 and 3. The script was rerun twice from scratch (`python3 line_e_frontier.py`, 3 min 11 s wall-clock, exit 0, `ruff check` and `ruff format --check` at line length 120 both pass); every number quoted in the notes was compared against the fresh stdout, and every displayed inequality in E1, E3, E5, E6, E7 and the Breakpoint was re-derived by hand.

## Verdict

- `refuted = false`, `fatal = false`. No PROVED-HERE claim in this line is *false*, and every number in E9a-E9e that the notes print is reproduced exactly by the script.
- But the line is materially overstated at three places where the notes' own hedges are dropped: the Goal and Results table say the push proves "the whole family of weighted-enstrophy Gronwall schemes is incapable" of reaching `g^2`, whereas Proposition E6 proves this only for the specific split-at-`N_0` scheme, only under the *unproved* hypothesis (D), and only for polynomial weights `h = N^a`; E4's two bulleted consequences are stated as an "if and only if" with an exact constant `ν/2` although they are read off a first-order expansion of a chain of `≍` relations whose error term is largest exactly where the conclusion is drawn; and the transfer table's `T2` row ("not applicable — no blowup mechanism is proposed") is wrong on its face, since E4 *is* a blowup mechanism carrying a blowup CONJECTURE, and that mechanism fails T2 against 3D viscous Burgers and against swirl-free axisymmetric flow.
- One statement in the transfer table is simply false as written (the 2.8 row's "in 2D the analogue of E1 holds at `s ≥ 1` by the same chain with `d = 2`": the chain cannot be run at `s = 1` in two dimensions).
- One COMPUTED transcription does not match the script (E9c's `p = 1.02` row) and one COMPUTED table silently drops the row that contradicts its own reading (E9e's `g = x^{0.05}`).
- Citation dependence is the heaviest lens here. The single organizing target of the whole line — that `g^2` is "BMR's condition" and that BMR proved it for a Navier-Stokes system — is assembled from two separate unread search snippets, one of which the notes themselves attribute to a *different* (dyadic) paper. Every positive result in the line (E1's globality, E5', E7) also rests on an [unverified] black box.

## Issues — major

### E6-scope — major — the obstruction is an obstruction to *one scheme*, not to "the whole family of weighted-enstrophy Gronwall schemes"

The Goal says the line proves "that the whole family of weighted-enstrophy Gronwall schemes is *incapable* of reaching Barbato-Morandin-Romito's `g^2` condition, because the weight cancels identically"; the Results table row E6 reads "**FAILED**; the obstruction is **PROVED-HERE**"; the Breakpoint calls it "a proved obstruction rather than an unfinished computation". What Proposition E6 actually proves is three steps weaker, and each of the three caveats is present in the body and absent from every summary. (a) It is conditional on hypothesis (D), which is *unproved* and which the notes themselves describe as "crude: it treats the trilinear form as if there were no cancellation". An obstruction derived from a deliberately cancellation-blind majorant is an obstruction to schemes that use that majorant, not to schemes that use the trilinear form. (b) The quantitative half — the sentence "the Osgood condition delivered by the scheme is `∫ds/(s g^4) = ∞` **for every such `h`**" — is proved only for `h(N) = N^a`, `a > -1`, plus the single extra example `h = g^2`; the standing hypotheses (H) admit far more weights than that, and the proof of E6 (taking logarithms of `N_0^{1+a}/g(N_0)^2 ≍ Y_h^{1/2}/ν`) uses the power form essentially. What makes the conclusion plausible for general `h` is the doubling constant `C_1` in (H), which forces `N h/g^2` to grow at most polynomially — but that argument is not made. (c) Even granting (a) and (b), the object shown to be unimprovable is the *pair* (estimate (D), split at `N_0`), and E7 shows only that one particular sharpening of (D) leaves the threshold alone. How verified: re-derived Proposition E6 and its proof; checked that the proof of the second sentence never uses (H) beyond `h = N^a`; checked that (D) is used in both branches of E5's split, so the coefficient `g(N_0)^4` cannot be separated from it. Accepted status: **PROVED-HERE for the split-at-`N_0` scheme under (H), (D) and `h = N^a`**; the "whole family" and "proved obstruction" phrasings are not supported and should be CONJECTURE.

### E4-iff — major — the two consequences of the cascade budget are read off a first-order expansion whose error is largest where the conclusion is drawn

Computation E4 is tagged PROVED-HERE with the parenthetical "as a scaling computation about the four displayed quantities; it is not a theorem about solutions". That parenthetical covers the four `≍` lines. It does not cover what follows. Two problems. (1) From `ΔE_j ≍ ν E_j^{1/2}/g(N_j)^2` — an equivalence with unnamed constants — the notes conclude "`E_j^{1/2}` decreases by `(ν/2)g(2^j)^{-2}` per stage" and then "the cascade reaches infinite frequency with energy left over ... **if and only if** `(ν/2)Σ_{j≥0}g(2^j)^{-2} < E_0^{1/2}`". A definite constant `ν/2` and a strict threshold cannot be extracted from `≍`; the implied constants are exactly what decides a borderline inequality between two finite numbers. (2) The expansion `E_{j+1}^{1/2} = E_j^{1/2} - ΔE_j/(2E_j^{1/2}) + O(ΔE_j^2/E_j^{3/2})` is valid only while `ΔE_j ≪ E_j`, and the second consequence ("it is drained at some finite stage for every initial energy and every `ν > 0` iff `Σ_j g(2^j)^{-2} = ∞`") is a statement about the regime `E_j → 0`, which is precisely where that expansion fails. The divergence/convergence *dichotomy* for `Σ g^{-2}` is robust to constants and survives; the two displayed iff statements do not. How verified: recomputed `ΔE_j/(2E_j^{1/2}) = ν/(2g(N_j)^2)` by hand and checked which step needs the dropped error term. Accepted status: the exponent arithmetic (that the relevant series is `Σ_j g(2^j)^{-2}`, and that at general `α` it is `Σ_j 2^{(2α-5/2)j}`) is **COMPUTED**; the two "if and only if" consequences are **CONJECTURE**.

### T2-E4 — major — the transfer table declares T2 "not applicable", but E4 is a blowup mechanism and it fails T2

The transfer check has the row "T2 comparison systems | not applicable | No blowup mechanism is proposed". This is not accurate. E4 explicitly proposes one: "consider the only blowup scenario that the energy identity plus scaling does not forbid: a *concentrating frequency cascade*", and it carries an explicit blowup CONJECTURE ("the converse, that `Σ_j g(2^j)^{-2} < ∞` permits an actual blowup"). Section 3's T2 therefore applies and must be run. Running it: Computation E4 uses only Bernstein, the energy identity and dimensional analysis, and none of these sees the pressure, incompressibility, or vortex stretching. Repeating the four lines for the 3D viscous Burgers system `∂_t u + (u·∇)u = νΔu` (no pressure, no divergence constraint) gives the identical amplitude `A_j ≍ N_j^{3/2}E_j^{1/2}`, the identical transfer time `T_j ≍ N_j^{-5/2}E_j^{-1/2}`, and the identical drain `ΔE_j/E_j^{1/2} ≍ νN_j^{-1/2}`, whose sum `ν Σ_j 2^{-j/2} = ν(2+√2)` is finite — so the budget "predicts" that the cascade is not drained for a system that is globally regular by the componentwise maximum principle (`00_problem.md` section 3, T2 list). The same holds verbatim for swirl-free axisymmetric 3D Navier-Stokes (item 2.9), which is globally regular but which the budget cannot distinguish from the general case, since no symmetry enters the four lines. The correct reading is the one the notes give for T1 and then do not apply here: E4 is a statement about what *the energy identity plus scaling cannot rule out*, and it has no predictive content about actual blowup. Consequently the word "natural" in "`g^2` is the *natural* threshold" and the sharpness CONJECTURE are both weaker than they read. How verified: re-ran the four E4 lines for Burgers and for the swirl-free reduction by hand; compared with the Breakpoint's own general-`α` computation, which is the same algebra. Accepted status: the T2 row must read "fails for E4"; E4's sharpness CONJECTURE stands only as a statement about the energy identity, not about the equation.

### 2.8-row — major — the transfer table's 2D claim is false as written

The 2.8 row reads: "The line proves regularity, not blowup; in 2D the analogue of E1 holds at `s ≥ 1` by the same chain with `d = 2` (not written out)." The chain cannot be run at `s = 1` in two dimensions. In `d = 2` the product estimate (K2) reads `‖fg‖_{Ḣ^{a+b-1}} ≤ C‖f‖_{Ḣ^a}‖g‖_{Ḣ^b}` under `a, b < d/2 = 1` and `a+b > 0`; step (3) requires output index `2-s`, hence `a+b = 3-s`, and both `a < 1` and `b < 1` force `a+b < 2`, i.e. `s > 1` strictly. The choice `a = 1` that E1 makes in `d = 3` is exactly the endpoint that is inadmissible in `d = 2`. (The scaling half of the claim is fine: redoing the degree computation with `d = 2` gives `d(σ) = 4s+2σ-4`, `d(s)+d(1) = 10s-6`, `d(1+s) = 6s-2`, equal iff `s = 1`.) So the threshold `s = 1` is right and the stated proof of it is wrong; and since 2D Navier-Stokes regularity at `s = 1` is item 2.8 itself, the row is asserting the very thing it is supposed to be tested against, by a route that does not work. How verified: redid steps (4)-(5) of E1 with `d = 2` and checked the admissible `(a,b)` region. Accepted status: the claim "`s ≥ 1` by the same chain" is **FAILED** at the endpoint; "`s > 1` by the same chain" would be CONJECTURE (not written out).

### BMR-attribution — major — the target of the entire line is assembled from two snippets, one of which is about a different paper

Everything in this line is organized around the proposition that the "natural"/"optimal" threshold is `∫ds/(s g^2) = ∞`, that Barbato-Morandin-Romito 2014 attained it for a Navier-Stokes system, and that the structure they used is a shell iteration. Three separate unverified inferences are stacked to get there, and none is flagged at the point of use. (1) E2b states flatly, as KNOWN, that BMR "proved global existence of smooth solutions 'under the optimal condition on the correction to the dissipation', proving that conjecture" — but the tag itself only confirms the abstract *phrase* "under the optimal condition", the *separate* fact that Tao's conjecture has the form `∫ds/(sG^2) = ∞`, and the title. That the paper's "optimal condition" is Tao's condition, rather than optimality in some other sense, is an inference from two snippets and is nowhere labelled as one. (2) The paper's title says "hyperdissipative Navier-Stokes **system**", and the notes' own companion citation is explicitly a *dyadic* equation; whether the 2014 A&PDE result is about the Navier-Stokes equations or about a model system was not checked, and the difference is decisive for E6 (an obstruction to schemes for the true equation is not in tension with a theorem about a dyadic model) and for E8. (3) E8 says the replacement structure "is, according to the search snippet quoted in E2b, exactly what Barbato-Morandin-Romito use" — but E2b's own bracket says that sentence "describes the companion dyadic paper". So E8 attributes to the 2014 A&PDE paper a method confirmed only for a different 2014 paper. Per `00_problem.md` section 0 rule 1, a claim may not rest on the precise hypotheses of an unread theorem without saying so at the point of use. How verified: read the three bracketed tags in E2b and E8 against the assertions they are attached to; no independent search was performed (the egress policy makes one pointless for verifying content). Accepted status: "BMR reached `∫ds/(s g^2) = ∞` for a Navier-Stokes system, by a shell iteration" is **CONJECTURE** in this project's epistemics, not KNOWN; every downstream sentence that treats `g^2` as "BMR's condition" or the shell iteration as "the structure that works in the literature" inherits that status.

### E9e-omit — major — a COMPUTED table drops the row that contradicts its own reading

The script's Part 4 Table B prints seven rows. The notes transcribe six: `g = x^{0.05}` (values `2.635, 3.140, 3.646, 4.156`, rate `0.1274`, ratio `1.27`) is absent. That row is the one that matters, because the accompanying reading says "the critical case `g = 1` sits at the inertial rate (ratio `0.91`, slightly damped) and the power losses sit well above it, so at 22 shells the model separates a power loss from criticality". With the omitted row restored, the power loss `x^{0.05}` sits at `1.27`, *between* the logarithmic cases `log^{1/2}` (`1.22`) and `log^{1}` (`1.46`) — i.e. the model does *not* separate power losses from logarithmic ones, and the sentence quoted is not supported by the table it summarizes. Separately, the diagnostic itself cannot do what the surrounding text implies. The reported quantity is `log10 max_t Σ_n λ^{2n}a_n^2` as a function of the truncation `M`, and for a perfectly regular constant-flux solution `a_n ≍ λ^{-βn/3}` that sum already diverges like `λ^{n/3}`, giving exactly the quoted `0.1003` decades per shell. So the "rate" measures the spectral slope of a divergent partial sum, not whether anything blows up, and "a larger rate signals an undamped cascade" (script) is a slope statement dressed as a blowup statement. No resolution-convergence check on the ODE tolerances was run, and no independent check that `max_t` is attained in the interior of `[0,60]`. How verified: diffed the notes' table against the fresh stdout row by row; recomputed the Kolmogorov balance `2α = 2β/3` and the resulting enstrophy divergence rate by hand. Accepted status: E9e is **COMPUTED** for the numbers, but the reading "the model separates a power loss from criticality" is **FAILED**; the notes' own final sentence ("not evidence for or against the divergence conditions") is the whole of what E9e supports.

### E1-K1 — major — Theorem E1 asserts globality, and globality is supplied by an unread citation

### E7-indep — major — "produced independently by two quite different trilinear estimates" is not what the computation shows

E7's Conclusion says the absorption threshold "is produced independently by two quite different trilinear estimates — the crude diagonal bound (D) and the commutator-improved transport bound — so it is not an artifact of a lossy estimate." The two derivations are not independent at the step that produces the threshold. In E5 the high-frequency branch converts `‖u_N‖_2 ≤ Y_h^{1/2}/(h(N)N)`; in E7 the low-mode sum is converted by `Σ_{M<N}(M^{3/2}/h(M))c_M ≤ (Σ_M M^{3/2}/h(M))·Y_h^{1/2}`. Both replace a local quantity by the *global* `Y_h^{1/2}`, and both then compare it against the dissipation at a single frequency. That shared substitution is exactly the lossy step that E8 goes on to blame ("the scheme compares a single scalar `Y_h(t)^{1/2}`, which knows only the total size of the solution, against the dissipation at each frequency separately"). So the agreement of the two thresholds is evidence that the *conversion* is what fixes them, which is the opposite of the stated conclusion. A second, smaller gap: E7 treats only the transport piece `⟨Λ^2u_N,(u_M·∇)u_N⟩` of the high-low-to-high interaction and asserts without argument that it is "the dominant high-frequency interaction"; the stretching piece `⟨Λ^2u_N,(u_N·∇)u_M⟩` is not written, and it is bounded by the same `‖∇u_M‖_∞N^2‖u_N‖_2^2` with no commutator available, so the conclusion survives but the computation as written does not cover the term that actually has no cancellation. How verified: re-derived both conversions and identified the common substitution; bounded the omitted stretching piece by hand.
