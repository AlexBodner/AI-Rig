## Verdict

- `refuted = false`, `fatal = false`. **No self-contained PROVED-HERE step is mathematically wrong.** B2 and B8 are correct identities (re-derived by hand and reproduced numerically with independent code); every exponent in every table is correct; the matched-tail cancellation of B6.1 and the piecewise CKN exponent are correct.

- The single most consequential finding: **the nonemptiness of the surviving window `2/5 <= beta < 1/2` carries no blowup information**, because the entire window arithmetic (B4, B5.2, B5.3, B5.4, B6, B10) applies verbatim to swirl-free axisymmetric 3D Navier–Stokes, which is globally regular (2.9). The transfer table records 2.9 as "not applicable"; the positive control T2 demands was never run, and running it deflates the headline sentence.
- The line is otherwise honest: the Breakpoint section names three real stopping points, and the FAILED sub-item inside B5 is recorded precisely as the house rules require.

## Issues

### B7.1 and the "Outcome in one line" — major — status inflation on B7.1 (not tag misuse on Chae–Wolf)

The headline asserts that "the only surviving self-similar blowup window for the Clay problem is `2/5 <= beta < 1/2`" and B7.1 is tagged PROVED-HERE. The strictness of the right endpoint is what makes the conclusion "Type II blowup, strictly faster than self-similar". For a profile in L3 that endpoint is closed by ESS (2.4) or NRS (2.3), both KNOWN. But the notes themselves identify the physically relevant profile as the matched one with tail `abs(U(y)) ~ abs(y)^(-1)` at `beta = 1/2`, which is *not* in L3 (the integral of `abs(y)^(-3)` diverges logarithmically), so ESS does not apply to it and NRS does not either. For that case the sole exclusion is Chae–Wolf 2017. Their precise hypotheses, and in particular their technical definition of "asymptotically self-similar", were never read. Per section 0 of `00_problem.md` a claim may not rest on the precise hypotheses of a cited theorem, so B7.1 cannot be PROVED-HERE. How verified: I re-derived that `norm(u(t), L3) = s^(2 beta - 1) norm(U, L3)` and that the matched tail at `beta = 1/2` gives `norm(U, L3) = infinity`, so the ESS route is genuinely unavailable there; and one WebSearch returned the abstract sentence "they remove a scenario of asymptotically self-similar blow-up for the Navier-Stokes equations with the profile belonging to `L^{p,infinity}(R^3)` with `p > 3/2`", which supports the bibliographic tag but not the hypotheses. The defect is therefore status inflation on B7.1, not a misuse of the citation tag on Chae–Wolf: one sentence of an abstract is exactly what section 0 permits that tag to carry. Accepted status: the arithmetic of the window is PROVED-HERE; B7.1 as a whole is KNOWN, and the right endpoint should be recorded as `beta <= 1/2` with `beta = 1/2` excluded only modulo the unread hypotheses.

### B3 "my proof, their lemma", and the 2.3 transfer row — major — the stated relation to NRS is not established

B3 says the content of NRS 1996 and Tsai 1998 "is precisely the missing lemma — that `U` in L3, or the local energy bounds of a suitable weak solution, force enough decay of `U` and `P` for the maximum-principle argument to run", and the 2.3 transfer row says 2.3 is "reproved in structure". Neither is supportable: nothing in this project establishes the implication "`U` in L3 implies (D0)–(D2)", and asserting that NRS's content is exactly that implication presupposes that their unread proof factors through this note's hypotheses. It may instead run through a completely different route (a Carleman or energy argument, a decay estimate not phrased through `Pi`). The honest statement is: B3 reaches `U == 0` from (D0)–(D2); NRS reach it from `U` in L3 by a route that was not read; the bridge is missing. How verified: re-read section 0's binding rule 1 and traced every hypothesis of B3 to its use. Accepted status: Theorem B3 PROVED-HERE exactly as stated under (D0)–(D2); the claim that it reproduces NRS's structure is CONJECTURE.

### B5.2, B5.3, B7.1 — major — "asymptotically self-similar" is used but never defined

B5.2 and B5.3 each end with a sentence extending the exclusion to the asymptotic case ("This kills asymptotically self-similar `beta > 1/2` too, not just the exact ansatz"), and B7.1's whole conclusion is about a class that by B4.2 cannot be exactly self-similar. But the note never says in what topology, on what region, and with what uniformity the approximation `u ~ s^(-alpha) U(x/s^beta)` is supposed to hold. Every norm identity used in B5.2, B5.3, B6.1 and the B10 rate table is an exact computation for the exact ansatz. For a solution that is only asymptotically of that form, `norm(u(t), L-infinity)`, `norm(grad u(t), L2)` and the CKN quantity are controlled by the leading term only if the correction is subordinate in exactly those norms, which is an assumption of the same strength as the conclusion. How verified: re-derived each norm identity and checked that each uses the exact ansatz globally in `y`. Accepted status: PROVED-HERE for the exact ansatz; CONJECTURE for every sentence extending an exclusion to the asymptotic class, until the class is defined.

### Transfer table row 2.9, and the T2 row — major — the required positive control was not run, and one stated reason is wrong

Row 2.9 reads "not applicable: this line does not use any symmetry reduction, so it neither uses nor is contradicted by the swirl-free result". That is the wrong verdict for a positive control. Swirl-free axisymmetric 3D Navier–Stokes is a subclass of 3D Navier–Stokes: it obeys the energy identity, Leray's lower bounds, CKN, and the same scaling, so B4.1, B4.2, B5.2, B5.3, B5.4, B6.1 and B10 apply to it verbatim and yield the identical nonempty window `2/5 <= beta < 1/2` — for a system that is KNOWN globally regular (2.9). The same is true for 3D viscous Burgers as far as B4.1, B5.4 and the B10 exponent arithmetic go. T2 is not literally violated, because a necessary condition is allowed to be vacuous; but the consequence is that **nonemptiness of the window is not evidence that anything survives**, and the headline sentence should not be read as a narrowing of the blowup problem. Separately, the T2 row's stated reason for viscous Burgers is wrong: the Lamb identity `(U.grad)U = grad(abs(U)^2/2) - U x curl U` holds for *any* smooth vector field, divergence-free or not, so B2 does not fail there; it fails at `Lap U = -curl omega` (which does need `div U = 0`) and at the absence of a pressure. How verified: applied B4–B6 and B10 to the swirl-free class step by step and found no line that uses swirl or vortex stretching; checked the Lamb identity for a non-divergence-free field symbolically. Accepted status: the transfer table rows 2.9 and T2 are FAILED as written and need rewriting.

### Script Part 5 and the T6 transfer row — major — the numerical check has no teeth

The T6 row says the profile identity "was re-derived with the dimension explicit, checked against the original energy identity, checked numerically (script Part 5, relative error `3.7e-10`)". Part 5 checks only `int ((y.grad)U).U dy = -(d/2) int abs(U)^2 dy`, which is an integration by parts that does **not** use `div U = 0` at all. The two steps that carry the content of the identity, `int ((U.grad)U).U = 0` and `int grad P . U = 0`, both of which need `div U = 0`, are never computed. How verified: I reran the same integral on an independent `80^3` grid with a deliberately non-divergence-free Gaussian-localized field and it held to relative error `2.3e-16` (max `abs(div U) = 2.45`), so the divergence-free construction in Part 5 is decorative; I then computed the two missing integrals independently on a `48^3` torus for a random Leray-projected field with the true Navier–Stokes pressure and obtained `-1.2e-14` and `4.1e-15`. The identity is true; the script does not check the part of it that needs a hypothesis. To be precise about what is and is not testable: both omitted steps are themselves generic in divergence-free fields, so the only profile-specific content of the identity — that `Res = 0` turns it into a constraint on `beta` — cannot be checked numerically without an actual solution, and none exists; the reasonable demand is only that Part 5 exercise `div U = 0` somewhere, which it does not. Accepted status: the identity COMPUTED (by me, not by the script as it stands); the T6 sentence overstates what Part 5 does.

### B9, Chae 2007 — major — the argument that the window survives turns on an unread quantifier

B9's reason that Chae's Euler nonexistence theorem "does not close the window" is that his hypothesis requires vorticity in `L^p` for *all* `p` in `(0, p_1)` for some `p_1 > 0`, while the window's matched vorticity tail `abs(y)^(-1/beta)` is in `L^p` only for `p > 3 beta >= 6/5`. The arithmetic is correct (I re-derived the tail exponent from `s`-independence of the outer vorticity and the convergence condition `p/beta > 3`). The quantifier is not. This is a claim about the precise hypothesis of a paper that was not read, and it is load-bearing in the *permissive* direction: it is the reason the line reports the window open rather than closed. My WebSearch returned only the weaker paraphrase "there exists no self-similar finite time blowing up solution to the 3D incompressible Euler equations if the vorticity decays sufficiently fast near infinity", plus the existence of a separate paper "A note on 'Nonexistence of self-similar singularities for the 3D incompressible Euler equations'" (arXiv math/0601661), which the notes do not mention and which may sharpen or correct the hypothesis. Accepted status: the tail arithmetic PROVED-HERE; "Chae's theorem does not apply" CONJECTURE.

### B10 — major — the hyperdissipative Leray lower bound is an unverified input inside a PROVED-HERE claim

B10 states "`beta <= beta_nu` is forced by the Leray/CKN arguments of B5" for general `sigma`, and supplies in parentheses a scaling-form lower bound `norm(u(t), L-infinity) >= s^(-(1 - beta_nu))` tagged "KNOWN [unverified as a reference, standard]". No such statement appears anywhere in the no-go checklist 2.1–2.15, which contains Leray's lower bounds only for `sigma = 1` (item 2.5), and it is not derived here. The whole "window empty for `sigma > (d+2)/4`" conclusion, which is the sharpest transfer claim in the line, therefore has an unverified and unlisted input. How verified: searched `00_problem.md` for any hyperdissipative lower bound (none), and traced B10's dependence on it. Accepted status: for `sigma = 1` PROVED-HERE given 2.5; for general `sigma` the upper edge `beta <= beta_nu` is CONJECTURE except for the exact ansatz, where B4.2's rigidity gives `beta = beta_nu` outright and is enough.

### B10 — minor — the COMPUTED tag adds nothing, and the CKN exponent is hard-coded

Script Part 4 contains no derivation: `e_exp`, the identity constant, `beta_nu = 1/(2 sigma)` and the piecewise CKN exponent `ckn = 2 - 1/bv if bv <= 1/2 else 6*bv - 3` are all typed in by hand and then printed. The notes nevertheless present the rate table as script output supporting the piecewise exponent, and the Breakpoint and transfer rows quote it as a computed fact. The exponents are in fact right — I confirmed `2 - 1/beta` and `6 beta - 3` independently in mpmath by evaluating `r^(-1) int_0^{r^2} s^(3 beta - 2) F(r s^(-beta)) ds` at `r = 1e-20` and `1e-24` for `beta` in `0.34, 0.35, 0.40, 0.45, 0.50, 0.55, 0.60`, matching the prediction to five decimals in every case — but a print statement is not a computation. Accepted status: PROVED-HERE for the arithmetic, COMPUTED should be dropped from B10 or the derivation moved into the script.

### B10 endpoint at `sigma = 5/4` — minor — a CONJECTURE is used as an input to a PROVED-HERE claim

The closure of the energy-critical endpoint reads "the complementary case of the matched tail ... is closed instead by the logarithmic divergence of B6.2". B6.2 is tagged CONJECTURE. The sub-argument actually used (the outer field `abs(x)^(-3/2)` is not locally square integrable in three dimensions, so the local energy diverges logarithmically) is sound on its own and could be retagged, but as the file stands a PROVED-HERE claim rests on a CONJECTURE. Separately, the dichotomy "either `U` in L2 or the exact matched power tail" is presented as exhaustive; it is exhaustive only among power-law tails. How verified: checked the tag of B6.2 and re-derived the divergence.

### B8 — minor — forward existence is used to explain backward nonexistence, and the scope is narrower than the heading

The closing sentence, "this is exactly why DSS solutions exist (Chae–Wolf; Bradshaw–Tsai, KNOWN, B9) while exactly self-similar ones do not", compares two different objects. B9 itself says the cited DSS solutions are *forward* solutions from singular data; and forward *exactly* self-similar solutions also exist (Jia–Šverák, cited in 2.3), so the contrast as drawn is false, and nothing is said about backward DSS, which is the case B8 is about. Separately, the heading says "discretely self-similar" and the text says "DSS/asymptotically self-similar", but Proposition B8 fixes the drift at `beta = 1/2` and `nu = 1`; the asymptotically self-similar profiles of the surviving window have `beta < 1/2` and a viscous coefficient `nu exp(-(1 - 2 beta) tau)`, so B8 does not literally cover them. How verified: compared B8's equation with the similarity-variable equation written in B7.3, and cross-read B9 and 2.3.

### B4.2 — minor — a hypothesis missing from the statement, and one offered proof route that does not work

The conclusion "an exactly self-similar Navier–Stokes solution must have `beta = 1/2`" is stated without hypotheses on `U`, but the final step needs `U` bounded (or of subpolynomial growth) to pass from `Lap U = 0` to `U == 0`; a harmonic polynomial profile is not excluded by anything written there. B5.1 does supply "for any bounded profile decaying at infinity", so the mathematics is fine, but B4.2 is quoted bare in B7.1. Also, the first of the two routes offered, "pairing `Lap U = 0` with `U` over `B_R` and letting `R -> infinity`", does not work: it produces `int_{B_R} abs(grad U)^2 = int_{boundary} U . d_n U`, and nothing in the hypotheses controls that boundary term. Only the parenthetical Liouville route is valid. How verified: carried out both routes by hand.

### B1, and the sentence about no-go item 2.3 — minor — the comparison is wrong, and it hides an error in `00_problem.md`

B1 says its profile equation "is the equation quoted in no-go item 2.3 with the opposite overall sign". It is not. Item 2.3 quotes `-Lap U - (1/2)U - (1/2)(y.grad)U + (U.grad)U + grad P = 0`; B1 derives `-Lap U + (1/2)U + (1/2)(y.grad)U + (U.grad)U + grad P = 0`. Multiplying either by `-1` gives an equivalent equation, so an "opposite overall sign" would be no difference at all; here only the two drift terms flip, which is a genuinely different equation — it is the *forward* self-similar profile equation, for `u = t^(-1/2) U(x/t^(1/2))`. B1's version is the correct backward one (I re-derived it from `s = T - t`, `d_t = -d_s`), so the error is in `00_problem.md` 2.3, and it matters: a reader who ran B3's maximum-principle argument with 2.3's drift would have `div b = -3/2 < 0` and Step 2 would fail. Recorded here because I own only this file; `00_problem.md` 2.3 needs the correction.

## Remarks that are not defects

- The B2 identity is genuinely generic: `U = curl A` with three arbitrary sympy functions does parametrize the smooth divergence-free fields on R3 by the Poincaré lemma, and the seven controls are real controls, not decoration. My independent spectral implementation agrees to `3.6e-12` and fails all seven controls. This is the strongest object in the line. A warning for any future reproducer: a naive spectral implementation on a torus gets the identity wrong by a factor of order `1e+03`, because `y . U` and `(y.grad)U` are not periodic; the non-periodic `y` factors must be differentiated by product rule with only the periodic factors sent through the FFT. The author's generic-sympy route sidesteps the trap entirely.
- B3's proof is careful in exactly the places where such proofs usually leak: the annulus bound notices that the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck drift contributes a bounded rather than a growing factor because `abs(y) <= 2R` on the support of the cutoff, and the monotone-cutoff trick correctly upgrades a `liminf` along a sequence to a statement on every ball.
- The FAILED sub-item in B5 is a model of the house style: the requested energy argument is written out, its actual reach (`beta < 2/5`) is stated, and the failure to reach `beta > 1/2` is recorded rather than dressed up.
- B7.3 is the right instinct and is correctly tagged CONJECTURE: the observation that `nu exp(-(1 - 2 beta) tau)` multiplies the highest-order operator, so the limit fails above wavenumber `exp((1 - 2 beta) tau / 2)`, is the correct objection to the whole route and the line refuses to argue past it.
- The remark that the ansatz is BKM-borderline for every `beta` because `alpha + beta = 1` is correct and is a genuinely useful observation: 2.7 gives no information about `beta`.
- The `beta = beta_nu` versus `beta_E` coincidence with the Lions exponent is correct but is not independent evidence: `beta_E <= beta_nu` unwinds to `4 sigma <= d + 2`, which is literally the energy-criticality computation of 1.6. The notes half-acknowledge this ("not a new proof of Lions' theorem"); they should acknowledge it fully.

## Transfer check

- **T1 (averaging).** Passes. The line contains no regularity argument for (A)/(B). The notes correctly name `Lap U = -curl omega`, the Lamb identity and the Riesz pressure as the exact-nonlinearity structure that B2, B3 and B8 use and that an averaged bilinear operator would not have.
- **T2 (globally regular comparison systems).** **Fails as recorded.** The 2D and hyperdissipative controls were run and reported correctly, but the swirl-free axisymmetric control (2.9) was not run and is recorded as "not applicable"; running it shows the window arithmetic transfers verbatim to a globally regular system, so nonemptiness of the window is not evidence of anything. The viscous Burgers reason given is also wrong (see the issue above). No blowup mechanism is proposed, so T2 is not violated; the table's verdicts are.
- **T3 (blowup profile constraints).** Passes for the exact ansatz. Every entry of the rate table is correct and every constraint of 2.4–2.7 is satisfied inside the window. For the asymptotically self-similar class the entries are unproved (see the "asymptotically self-similar" issue).
- **T4 (inviscid limit).** Passes. The mandatory scaling check of the viscous term against the stretching term is done (`Re(s) = s^(2 beta - 1)/nu`), no claim is uniform in `nu -> 0`, and Elgindi's exponent is honestly left unrecalled rather than guessed.
- **T5 (weak solutions).** Passes. Everything is for a classical solution smooth on `[0, T*)`, and the places where smoothness is used are named.
- **T6 (too-easy test).** Triggered once and mostly honoured, but the numerical half of the honouring is empty (see the Part 5 issue), and B10's `sigma > 5/4` emptiness is a cheaper-looking closure than it is, because of the unverified hyperdissipative lower bound.

## Citation dependence

Three claims depend on the precise hypotheses of papers no one in this project has read, and none of the three says so in the language section 0 requires. (i) **Chae–Wolf 2017** carries the exclusion of `beta = 1/2` for the non-L3 matched profile, hence the strict right endpoint of the headline window; a WebSearch snippet does reproduce the abstract sentence about `L^{p,infinity}` with `p > 3/2`, so the bibliographic tag is defensible, but the paper's technical definition of "asymptotically self-similar" and any further hypotheses are unknown, and B7.1 uses the result as if fully known. (ii) **Chae 2007** carries, in the permissive direction, the claim that the window is *not* already closed; that argument needs the exact quantifier "for all `p` in `(0, p_1)`", which my search did not reproduce, and there exists an unmentioned "A note on ..." paper about the same result. (iii) The **hyperdissipative Leray lower bound** used in B10 is tagged "[unverified as a reference, standard]" and is not in the no-go checklist, yet supports the line's sharpest transfer claim. Two further items are handled correctly and should be the model: Chae's asymptotically-self-similar paper ("hypotheses not recalled and deliberately not guessed") and Elgindi's exponent ("[unverified, not recalled]").
