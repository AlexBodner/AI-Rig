## Verdict

- `refuted = false`, `fatal = false`. The mathematical core is sound: D1 is a correct identity, D2's chain of inequalities is correct step by step, D3(a)-(d) are correct, D3b is correct, D6 is correct, and every computed number reproduces exactly.

- The line is honest in intent — its Breakpoint section is precise and its self-deprecating remarks (D2 reproves Kato; D7 is weaker than ESS; D6 transfers to the averaged equation) are correct — but several of its summary sentences are stronger than the mathematics below them.

## Issues

### 1. D4(d), D4(e), Goal, Breakpoint B1 — major — the upper end of the `κ_sharp` bracket is a `T^3` number and `κ_sharp` is an `R^3` infimum

`κ_sharp := inf_u Θ(u)` is defined in D3 over "smooth Schwartz divergence-free `u ≢ 0` on `R^3`". Every number in Part 3 and Part 4 of the script is an integral over `T^3 = (R/2πZ)^3` of a trigonometric polynomial, which is not in that class (its `L^3(R^3)` norm is infinite). The script's own summary line, the D4(d) display `kappa: 0.166695 (proved) / 0.206993 ≤ kappa_sharp ≤ 151.97771 (optimized, on T^3)`, the Goal's "a computed upper bound on the sharp threshold that brackets `κ`", D4(d)'s "the constant `κ = 0.167 ν` is a proved threshold whose distance from the truth is unknown to within at least a factor of `10^2`", and B1's "D4 brackets `κ_sharp` only between `0.167ν` and `152ν`" all assert an inequality between an `R^3` quantity and a `T^3` quantity with no transference argument. This is not a pedantic point: `Θ` is *not* asymptotically transferable. If one truncates the periodic optimizer `v` with a cutoff at scale `L`, then `D` and `P` both grow like `L^3` while `‖u‖_{L^3}` grows like `L`, so `Θ ≈ Θ_{T^3} · (L/2π) → ∞`. The `T^3` value is therefore the `L ≈ one period` end of a family that diverges, and the only way to make it an upper bound for `κ_sharp` is an explicit estimate of the order-one damage the cutoff does to `D`, `P` and `‖u‖_3` — including the possibility that it flips the sign of `P` — which is nowhere attempted. Contrast the treatment of `C_p` in the same table, where a transference theorem is at least invoked. Verified by rerunning Part 3/Part 4 (all digits match) and by carrying out the truncation scaling by hand. **Accepted status: COMPUTED, as a statement about a torus functional only; there is currently no upper bound of any kind on `κ_sharp`, and `κ_sharp < ∞` for the `R^3` functional is not established (see issue 2).**

### 2. D3 prose and Breakpoint B1 — major — `κ_sharp < ∞` rests on numerics alone, and the cross-reference to D3b is wrong

D3 states: "`K* ≤ 0` is false: D3b exhibits (and D4 computes) fields with `P(u) > 0`. So `κ_sharp < ∞` and the `L^3` monotonicity method has a genuine, finite ceiling." D3b does not exhibit a field with `P(u) > 0`; D3b proves `P(u) = 0` on an infinite-dimensional class, which is the opposite. The only support for `K* > 0`, hence for `κ_sharp < ∞`, is the computed `P(v) = 0.001645` in Part 3/4 — a grid integral of a non-band-limited integrand. (I confirmed independently that `P ≠ 0` is numerically robust: on my own basis and coefficients, `P = +8.7412e-05` on the `abs(k)^2 ≤ 2` shells, stable to five significant digits at `N = 32, 64, 96` while `P` on the `abs(k) = 1` shell sits at `1e-20`. So the fact is almost certainly true, and one exact symbolic evaluation on a two-shell field would settle it.) But as the notes stand, a claim carried as the negative headline of a PROVED-HERE section ("`κ_sharp < ∞`", "the method has a genuine, finite ceiling"), and repeated flatly in Breakpoint B1 as "`κ_sharp < ∞` because `K* > 0` (D3d, exhibited in D4e)", has only COMPUTED support — and D3(d) as actually stated proves `κ_sharp > 0`, not `κ_sharp < ∞`. Verified by reading D3b and D3(d) against the sentence, and by the independent shell computation. **Accepted status: COMPUTED (on `T^3`); PROVED-HERE is not earned, and by issue 1 the `R^3` version is not established at all.**

### 3. D3(e) — major — the claim quantifies over arguments, is not a proposition, and on its literal reading is false

"(e) No rescaling, covering, or induction-on-scales argument enlarges the set in (b)" is tagged PROVED-HERE. Its "proof" is: "Suppose an argument covered the data set by rescaled copies. By (a) every quantity entering (iv) is scale-invariant, so the differential inequality at scale `λ` is the *same* inequality." That establishes exactly one thing, which is true and worth saying: the criterion `‖u^0‖_3 < κ` and the differential inequality (iv) are invariant under `u ↦ λu(λ·)`, so applying the scaling symmetry to a datum changes nothing. It does not, and cannot, establish a statement about the class of all conceivable arguments, because that class is not a mathematical object. On the literal reading the sentence is also false: the set of data for which global regularity is known *is* strictly larger than `{‖u^0‖_3 < κ}` — Koch-Tataru smallness in `BMO^{-1}` (`00_problem.md` item 2.15) covers data of arbitrarily large `L^3` norm — and localization-plus-induction-on-scales is precisely the shape of the CKN `ε`-regularity argument (item 2.6), which is a covering argument that yields information for large data. The honest content of D3 is (a)-(d) plus the observation that the *bracket* `θ‖u‖_3 - 8ν/3` has a fixed sign structure under both scalings; that is the supercriticality barrier instance, and it is enough. Verified by reading the proof of (e) and checking that no step of it mentions any object other than `K`, `Θ` and the scalings. **Accepted status: CONJECTURE (or, better, a remark); PROVED-HERE is false as tagged, since nothing is proved.**

### 6. D5 — major — the written rescaling does not produce a *bounded* ancient solution, so the stated bridge to D6/D7 does not exist

D5 sets `U_k(y,s) := λ_k u(x_0 + λ_k y, T* + λ_k^2 s)` and then says: "The Type I bound gives `abs(U_k(y,s)) ≤ C abs(s)^{-1/2}` uniformly in `k`, so on each parabolic cylinder `{abs(s) ≥ ε}` the family is uniformly bounded; ... a diagonal extraction produces a limit `U` which is a bounded mild ancient solution on `R^3 × (-∞,0)`." The two clauses contradict each other. Centering the rescaling at `T*` gives exactly `abs(U_k(y,s)) ≤ λ_k C (T* - (T* + λ_k^2 s))^{-1/2} = C abs(s)^{-1/2}`, which diverges as `s ↑ 0`, so the limit satisfies `abs(U(y,s)) ≤ C abs(s)^{-1/2}` and is *not* bounded on `R^3 × (-∞,0)`. That matters, and is not a wording slip: `A := sup_{s<0}‖U(s)‖_{L^∞} < ∞` is a hypothesis of D6 and of D7, and "bounded mild ancient solution" is the defining property of the KNSS class named in the Setting. As written, D5 therefore does not deliver an object to which D6, D7 or the KNSS theorems quoted in D5(a),(b) apply, and the whole Type I programme of this line has no stated input. (The repair is one line — center at `t_k ↑ T*` with `λ_k = (T* - t_k)^{1/2}`, which gives `abs(U_k) ≤ C(1-s)^{-1/2} ≤ C` on `s < 0` — but it is not what is written, and the reduction is tagged KNOWN on the strength of a Seregin-Šverák paper marked "[unverified]", so the printed derivation is all the reader has.) Verified by recomputing the Type I bound under the stated rescaling. **Accepted status: KNOWN only as an attribution to the cited literature; the derivation printed here is invalid, and nothing in this file connects a Type I singularity to the hypotheses of D6 or D7.**

### 7. D7 step (3) — major — the appeal to Tychonov uniqueness is a non sequitur

The Duhamel representation of `Ũ` — the identity from which the entire conclusion `c'(s) = -g(s)` is extracted — is justified as follows: "the difference between `Ũ` and the right-hand side below solves the heat equation with zero data and lies in `L^∞ + L^{3/2} + L^3` uniformly on `[s_0,s]`, hence has at most Gaussian growth; Tychonov uniqueness applies in that class". Membership in `L^{3/2} + L^3` implies no pointwise bound whatsoever, let alone `abs(v(x,t)) ≤ C e^{a abs(x)^2}`, which is what the Tychonov class requires; and the one term whose pointwise size is genuinely unclear is the only one that made the author reach for `L^{3/2} + L^3` in the first place, namely `∫ e^{(s-τ)Δ} ρ(· + h(τ),τ) dτ` (the naive bound `‖e^{tΔ}ρ_1‖_∞ ≤ ‖G_t‖_3 ‖ρ_1‖_{3/2} = ct^{-1}‖ρ_1‖_{3/2}` is not integrable in `τ`). So the step as written does not establish the representation formula. I believe the formula is true and that differentiating `e^{(s-τ)Δ}Ũ(τ)` in `τ` and integrating gives it directly in `L^3 + L^{3/2} + L^∞`, but that is a different argument and it is not the one in the file. Everything downstream — `c' = -g`, `∇p̃ = ρ`, step (4), step (5) — rests on this representation. Verified by checking what the stated hypotheses give pointwise. **Accepted status: the D7 conclusion is plausible and each of steps (1),(2),(4),(5) checks out, but PROVED-HERE is not earned by the text as written; the tag should be conditional on rewriting step (3).**

### 8. "Setting and notation", used by D1 and D2 — major — the standing regularity/decay class is not preserved by the Navier-Stokes flow

The standing hypothesis is that `u` is "Schwartz in `x` for each `t` (the class fixed in `00_problem.md` §4)", and D1's proof discharges every convergence and every integration by parts with the single sentence "All the integrands below are continuous and decay rapidly, so every integral converges and every integration by parts on `R^3` has no boundary term." But the Schwartz class is not invariant under Navier-Stokes: the nonlocal pressure destroys rapid decay instantaneously, and the far-field velocity decays only polynomially (this project's own `refutations/line_a_r1_rigor.md` records the Brandolese and Dobrokhotov-Shafarevich attributions for the `abs(x)^{-4}` rate, with the rate itself unverified). Nothing in this project proves that any nontrivial solution stays Schwartz for `t > 0`, so as literally stated the standing class may be empty for `t > 0` and D2 — whose hypothesis is only that `u^0` is Schwartz — then applies D1 to a solution that does not satisfy D1's hypotheses. The damage is repairable, since every integral in D1 converges under the decay actually available (`abs(u) ~ abs(x)^{-4}`, `p ~ abs(x)^{-3}` give `abs(u)abs(∇u)^2 ~ abs(x)^{-14}` and `p u ∇abs(u) ~ abs(x)^{-12}`), but the hypothesis stated is the wrong one and the justification given ("decay rapidly") is unavailable. This defect is inherited from `00_problem.md` §4 and affects any line that adopts that class. Verified by checking which decay each integral in D1 and D2 actually needs. **Accepted status: PROVED-HERE for D1 and for D2's a priori estimate once the standing class is restated as the decay the flow actually propagates; as written the hypothesis is unverified for `t > 0`.**

### 10. D4(d) — major — de Leeuw transference is applied to a symbol that is discontinuous at a lattice point

"The `‖p‖_3/‖abs(u)^2‖_3` column is a lower bound for the true `R^3` operator norm `C_p` by de Leeuw's transference theorem (the periodic multiplier norm of a continuous symbol is at most its `R^3` multiplier norm; KNOWN [unverified])." The symbol in question is `-ξ_iξ_j/abs(ξ)^2`, which is homogeneous of degree zero and *not* continuous at `ξ = 0` — and `0` is a lattice point, exactly the point where the restriction `m abs_{Z^3}` is being taken. The parenthetical states the hypothesis of the theorem being invoked and then applies it to a symbol that fails it. There is a standard workaround (the zero mode is discarded because `p` is normalized to zero mean, and the symbol is continuous at every other lattice point), and the operator here is matrix-valued, so a vector-valued form of the theorem is also needed; neither is discussed, and by `00_problem.md` §0 the paper was never read, so no version of the hypotheses is available. This is non-load-bearing — `C_p ≥ 0.398` is used only to say that the truth lies somewhere in `[0.398, 18.72]` and nothing depends on it — but the lens is mandatory. Verified by inspecting the symbol at `ξ = 0` against the stated hypothesis. **Accepted status: COMPUTED as a lower bound for the `T^3` constant; CONJECTURE as a lower bound for the `R^3` constant `C_p`.**

### 11. D2 step (v) — minor — "`T_1 > 0` by continuity" does not follow

With `N_0 := ‖u^0‖_3` and `T_1 := sup{t < T* : ‖u(s)‖_3 ≤ N_0 for all s ≤ t}`, the threshold is attained at `t = 0`, so continuity of `t ↦ ‖u(t)‖_3` gives nothing: an arbitrarily small increase at `t = 0^+` would make `T_1 = 0` and the bootstrap would never start. (The argument is rescuable — either take a slack level `N_1 ∈ (N_0, κ)` and run the same continuity argument there, or note that step (iv) at `t = 0` already gives `Y'(0) ≤ -δ X(0)^2 < 0` for `u^0 ≢ 0` — but neither is what is written.) This is the same genre as the A4 finding in `refutations/line_a_r1_rigor.md`: the conclusion is true, the written justification does not deliver it. Verified by attempting to derive `T_1 > 0` from continuity alone. **Accepted status: PROVED-HERE once the bootstrap is restated.**

### 12. D6, input (O) — minor — the Oseen kernel is not rapidly decaying

"the kernel of `e^{tΔ} P div` is `t^{-2} G(x/sqrt t)` with `G` smooth and rapidly decaying, so its `L^{q'}` norm is `C_q t^{-2 + 3/(2q')}`". The scaling form and the exponent arithmetic are right (I re-derived `-2 + 3/(2q') = -1/2 - 3/(2q)`), but `G` is not rapidly decaying: the Leray projector has a symbol that is homogeneous of degree zero and singular at the origin, so `G` decays only polynomially, `abs(G(y)) ≲ (1 + abs(y))^{-4}`. The conclusion survives untouched, because `4q' > 3` for every `q' ≥ 1`, so `‖G‖_{q'} < ∞` for all `q ∈ [1,∞]` and both uses (`q = 2`, `q = 6`) are fine. Only the stated reason is wrong. Verified by inspecting the symbol. **Accepted status: KNOWN, with the decay rate restated.**

### 13. D7 — minor — three loose ends in the hypotheses and the statement

(a) Step (1) concludes `h = c(s)` with `c` "smooth in `s`". Hypothesis (i) controls only `sup_s ‖Ω(s)‖_{L^{3/2}}` and says nothing about `∂_s Ω`, so the `s`-regularity of `V = K * Ω` — and hence of `c = U - V` — is not justified; steps (2) and (3) need `c'` to exist. (b) The Setting asserts "such `U` are smooth with all derivatives bounded, KNOWN" with no citation tag at all, in violation of `00_problem.md` §0, and this is load-bearing twice in D7 (for `V` bounded in step (1) and for `∇Ũ ∈ L^∞` in step (4)). (c) The final clause "If moreover `g ≡ 0` then `U` is a constant vector" is vacuous inside the class the theorem is stated for: if `U(x,s) = b(s)` is spatially constant then `U ⊗ U` is constant, `P div(U ⊗ U) = 0`, `e^{tΔ}b = b`, and the mild formula forces `b(s) = b(s_0)` for all `s_0 < s`, i.e. `b` constant and `g ≡ 0` automatically. The `b(s)` solutions with `b' = -g ≠ 0` live in the classical class, not the mild class the theorem assumes. Verified by substituting a spatially constant field into the mild formulation. **Accepted status: PROVED-HERE with (a) and (b) supplied and (c) deleted or restated for classical solutions.**

### 14. Goal paragraph, D4(d) wording, D4(b) — minor — three presentation overclaims

(a) The Goal reads "so a Liouville theorem for that class excludes Type I blowup. I prove two Liouville theorems (one in `L^∞_s L^3_x`, one in `L^∞_s L^{3/2}_x` for the vorticity)". Both theorems require *smallness* in those norms, and the qualifier does not appear until two sentences later; an unconditional Liouville theorem in `L^∞_s L^3_x` would be ESS-strength and would be a major result. The same paragraph is otherwise scrupulous ("Nothing here solves or claims to solve the Clay problem"), which makes the omission more conspicuous, not less. (b) D4(d) says "`Θ` is minimized over the coefficients"; what the script performs is one or two random restarts of Nelder-Mead with `maxiter = 1200` in 36 dimensions and a single Powell run with `maxfev = 2200` in 52 dimensions, which is a partial descent, not a minimization. (c) D4(b) asserts "Both columns converge with `N`", and the transport column does not decrease monotonically (`5.1e-05` at `N = 48`, `-5.8e-05` at `N = 64` in the zero-mean block); the relative-error column does. **Accepted status: unchanged, wording only.**
