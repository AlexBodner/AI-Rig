## Issues

### A4 — major — the shrinking-bump argument as written does not prove the `L^3` statement

The claim is "no function of the energy controls `‖u‖_{L^3}` (or the enstrophy) on `T^3`", and the family offered is `u_λ(x) = λ u_0(λ(x − x_0))` with `∫|u_λ|^3` fixed, `∫|u_λ|^2 = λ^{-1}∫|u_0|^2 → 0`, `∫|∇u_λ|^2 → ∞`. This proves the enstrophy half (an energy sublevel set contains fields of arbitrarily large enstrophy). It does not prove the `L^3` half: along `u_λ` the `L^3` norm is constant, so nothing prevents a nondecreasing `Φ` with `Φ(E) ≥ Ψ(‖u‖_3)` from holding on this family (`Φ(0^+) ≥ Ψ(‖u_0‖_3)` is no contradiction). What is needed is a family with bounded energy and unbounded `L^3` norm, e.g. `λ^{1/2} u_λ` (energy `= ∫|u_0|^2` fixed, `∫|λ^{1/2}u_λ|^3 = λ^{3/2}∫|u_0|^3 → ∞`). The same one-line fix is needed in the A6 Corollary sentence "`Q_k ≤ C(1 + ‖u‖_2^2)`, and by the shrinking-bump argument of A4 no such `Q` controls `‖u‖_{L^3}`". Verified by re-reading the argument and checking the scalings by hand. The claim is true; the written proof is incomplete, so the PROVED-HERE tag is currently too strong for the `L^3` part.

### A6-certificate — minor — certificate not self-contained in the notes

The notes state that the five fields `w^{(1)}, ..., w^{(5)}` have "coefficients printed", but only the `N(w^{(m)})` rows and the determinant appear in `line_a_lyapunov.md`; the coefficients are printed by the script only. A reader of the notes cannot recompute the rows. (I recomputed them from the script output with independent code and they are correct.) Paste the five coefficient lines into A6(4).

### A6-theorem / Corollary — minor — `C^2` hypothesis excludes `∫|u|^p` for `1 < p < 2`

The theorem assumes `φ ∈ C^2(R^3)`; the corollary then lists "`∫|u|^p` (`p ≠ 2`)" among the excluded functionals, but `|v|^p` is not `C^2` at `v = 0` for `p < 2`. The proof still works because the perturbation `u_ε = v_0 + εw` with `v_0 ≠ 0` and `ε‖w‖_∞ < |v_0|` keeps `u_ε` away from the origin, where `|v|^p` is smooth, and step (1) (integration by parts of `∂_iφ(u)∂_ip`) needs `φ ∈ C^2` only along the range of `u_ε`. This should be said; as written the corollary quotes a theorem whose hypothesis fails. Verified by checking where `C^2` is used in steps (1)–(4).

### A5-reduction (A5.2) — minor — integrability of `log|u|` is assumed silently for general `u ∈ V`

The identification `L(u)[B(u,u)] = 2Π(u)` integrates by parts with the vector field `log|u| u`, and the asymptotic constant `C_1 = ∫ log|u| |∇u|^2` is used in the four-term prediction; both need `log|u| ∈ L^1(T^3)`, i.e. `log|u| u ∈ W^{1,1}`. For a smooth `u` with a flat zero (`|u| ~ exp(−|x|^{-4})` near a point) `log|u| ∉ L^1_{loc}` in three dimensions, and the a.e. divergence of `log|u| u` need not be its distributional divergence. For the real-analytic trigonometric polynomial `w` of A5.3 everything is fine (Łojasiewicz), and A1c' only needs (H3'') at `±w`, which follows from dominated convergence without any integration by parts; moreover the script's direct computation `Euler flux / A^3 → 335.3 = 2Π` bypasses the identity. So the conclusion "`∫|u|^2 log(1+|u|)` is not a Lyapunov functional" is unaffected; A5.2 should be stated for `u` with `log|u| ∈ L^1` (or for trigonometric polynomials). Verified by tracing the regularity needed at each integration by parts.

### A3.3 — minor — the hedge is on the wrong point, and K4 as stated does not literally apply

The notes say "uniqueness of the maximizer is numerically supported, not proved". Uniqueness is not needed: Danskin gives `Q'(u;h) = max_{x ∈ M} u(x)·h(x)/|u(x)|`, so the one-sided Navier–Stokes derivative at data `Au` is at least `A G + A^2 F` evaluated at any *global* maximizer `x*`, and positivity there already violates (H2). What is not certified is that the point found by the `48^3` grid search plus BFGS is a global maximizer (a Lipschitz bound `|∇|u|| ≤ Σ_n 4π|n||a_n|` on the grid spacing would certify it against the printed gap `20.65` vs `18.85`). Separately, K4 requires `∂_s f` continuous on `K × (−ε,ε)`, which fails for `f(x,s) = |u(x) + s h(x)|` at zeros of `u`; apply Danskin to `|u + sh|^2` and take the square root. My independent `96^3` search reproduces `x*`, the maximum and both fluxes to all printed digits, and the near-maximal grid points form one cluster, so the numbers stand.

Verified with a small script that replays the seeded generator and uses the exact `Fraction` path.

## Remarks that are not defects

- A1 is stronger than advertised: conclusion (a) (Euler invariance) needs (H2) only for data with `Q(u^0) ≥ M` (large amplitude), which is the class relevant to a priori bounds ("Lyapunov outside a ball"); only (b) uses small amplitudes. The notes do not claim this, so no status is affected.
- The `L^p` candidates with `p < 2` and `∫|ω|^{3/2}` satisfy (H1) (remainder `O(|h|^p)` resp. `O(|h|^{3/2})` pointwise, `o(‖h‖_∞)`), so A1 applies to them as used.
- The `16^3` exactness claim in A3.2 is correct: the highest-degree integrand (`|u|^2 u·B`) has degree `5` per variable and all intermediate products (`u ∂u`, `u_iu_j`) have degree `2 < 8`.
- The threshold-optimization result (`A*‖u‖_2` from `424` to `63.5`) is correctly labelled as not a bound.
