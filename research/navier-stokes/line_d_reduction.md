# Line D — Conditional reductions: the `L^3` wall and the ancient-solution Liouville problem

## Goal

Two reductions of Clay (A)/(B) to sharply stated lemmas, each attempted to the point where it breaks. (1) The **`L^3` wall**: by Escauriaza–Seregin–Šverák (`00_problem.md` item 2.4) any blowup drives `‖u(t)‖_{L^3}` to infinity, so it suffices to bound the critical Lebesgue norm. I compute `d/dt ‖u‖_{L^3}^3` exactly, isolate the dissipation and the pressure work, and close the estimate with an explicit numerical threshold `κ` — then prove precisely why the same computation can never reach large data. (2) The **ancient-solution Liouville problem**: Seregin–Šverák rescaling converts a Type I blowup into a nontrivial bounded mild ancient solution on `R^3 × (−∞,0)`, so a Liouville theorem for that class excludes Type I blowup. I prove two Liouville theorems (one in `L^∞_s L^3_x`, one in `L^∞_s L^{3/2}_x` for the vorticity), and record where a third attempt — the Constantin–Fefferman vorticity-direction hypothesis — fails.

Nothing here solves or claims to solve the Clay problem. What is produced is: an exact identity, one explicit number `κ ≈ 0.167 ν` with every constant named and every loss located, a computed upper bound on the sharp threshold that brackets `κ`, an explicit velocity field at which `‖u‖_{L^3}` increases, two complete Liouville theorems, and one documented failure.
