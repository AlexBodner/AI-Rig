# Line D — Conditional reductions: the `L^3` wall and the ancient-solution Liouville problem

## Goal

Two reductions of Clay (A)/(B) to sharply stated lemmas, each attempted to the point where it breaks. (1) The **`L^3` wall**: by Escauriaza–Seregin–Šverák (`00_problem.md` item 2.4) any blowup drives `‖u(t)‖_{L^3}` to infinity, so it suffices to bound the critical Lebesgue norm. I compute `d/dt ‖u‖_{L^3}^3` exactly, isolate the dissipation and the pressure work, and close the estimate with an explicit numerical threshold `κ` — then prove precisely why the same computation can never reach large data. (2) The **ancient-solution Liouville problem**: Seregin–Šverák rescaling converts a Type I blowup into a nontrivial bounded mild ancient solution on `R^3 × (−∞,0)`, so a Liouville theorem for that class excludes Type I blowup. I prove two Liouville theorems (one in `L^∞_s L^3_x`, one in `L^∞_s L^{3/2}_x` for the vorticity), and record where a third attempt — the Constantin–Fefferman vorticity-direction hypothesis — fails.

Nothing here solves or claims to solve the Clay problem. What is produced is: an exact identity, one explicit number `κ ≈ 0.167 ν` with every constant named and every loss located, a computed upper bound on the sharp threshold that brackets `κ`, an explicit velocity field at which `‖u‖_{L^3}` increases, two complete Liouville theorems (one of them under an explicitly stated pressure normalization), and one documented failure.

## Setting and notation

Notation follows `00_problem.md` §0. Throughout, `u` is a smooth solution of (1)-(3) with `f ≡ 0`, `ν > 0`, on `R^3 × [0,T*)`, Schwartz in `x` for each `t` (the class fixed in `00_problem.md` §4; strictly stronger than Fefferman's (6)-(7), so uniqueness is available). `p = R_i R_j (u_i u_j)` is the Leray pressure, `R_j = ∂_j (−Δ)^{-1/2}` the Riesz transforms, `ω = curl u`. Write

```text
Y(t) = ∫ |u(x,t)|^3 dx = ‖u(t)‖_{L^3}^3,     w = |u|^{3/2},     X(t) = ‖∇ w(t)‖_{L^2},
D(u) = 3 ∫ |u| |∇u|^2 dx + 3 ∫ |u| |∇|u||^2 dx,     P(u) = 3 ∫ p (u·∇|u|) dx.
```

`X^2 = (9/4) ∫ |u| |∇|u||^2` (chain rule), so `D(u) = 3 ∫ |u||∇u|^2 + (4/3) X^2`. Under the Navier-Stokes scaling `u_λ(x,t) = λ u(λx, λ^2 t)` of `00_problem.md` §1.6: `Y` is invariant, `X_λ = λ X`, `D(u_λ) = λ^2 D(u)`, `P(u_λ) = λ^2 P(u)`. Under the amplitude scaling `u ↦ a u` (a fixed field, not a solution): `D(au) = a^3 D(u)` but `P(au) = a^4 P(u)` — the two are of different homogeneity, which is the whole story of D3. Also `P(−u) = −P(u)` while `D(−u) = D(u)`, since `p` is quadratic (even) and `u·∇|u|` is odd.

For ancient solutions, `s` denotes time on `(−∞,0)`, `U` the solution, `Ω = curl U`. A *bounded mild ancient solution* is a bounded `U` on `R^3 × (−∞,0)` with `U(s) = e^{ν(s−s_0)Δ} U(s_0) − ∫_{s_0}^{s} e^{ν(s−τ)Δ} P div(U ⊗ U) dτ` for all `s_0 < s < 0`, `P` the Leray projector (this is the class of Koch-Nadirashvili-Seregin-Sverak; such `U` are smooth with all derivatives bounded, KNOWN).

### D1. The exact `L^3` identity — **PROVED-HERE**

**Theorem D1.** For a smooth solution as above, for every `t ∈ (0,T*)`,

```text
d/dt ∫ |u|^3 dx = −3ν ∫ |u| |∇u|^2 dx − 3ν ∫ |u| |∇|u||^2 dx + 3 ∫ p (u·∇|u|) dx
                = −ν D(u) + P(u),
```

and the second dissipation term in the `|u|^{3/2}` form is `3ν ∫ |u| |∇|u||^2 = (4ν/3) ∫ |∇(|u|^{3/2})|^2 = (4ν/3) X^2`. The transport term contributes exactly zero. Consequently `D(u) ≥ (8/3) X^2`, since `|∇u|^2 ≥ |∇|u||^2` pointwise gives `3 ∫|u||∇u|^2 ≥ 3·(4/9) X^2 = (4/3) X^2`.

*Proof.* (1) *Chain rule at the zeros of `u`.* `|u|^3 = (|u|^2)^{3/2}` and `s ↦ s^{3/2}` is `C^1` on `[0,∞)`, so `|u|^3 ∈ C^1` in `(x,t)` with `∂_t |u|^3 = 3|u| u·∂_t u` and `∂_j |u|^3 = 3 |u| u_i ∂_j u_i`, both continuous. Likewise `|u| u_i` is `C^1`: away from `{u = 0}` this is clear, and at a zero `x_0`, `| |u| u_i |(x_0 + h) ≤ L^2 |h|^2 = o(|h|)` with `L` a local Lipschitz constant for `u`, so it is differentiable there with derivative `0`, and `∂_j(|u| u_i) = |u| ∂_j u_i + u_i ∂_j |u|` is continuous because `|u_i ∂_j|u|| ≤ |u| |∇u| → 0`. Finally `w = |u|^{3/2}` is `C^1` with `∇w = (3/2)|u|^{1/2} ∇|u|` (and `= 0` on `{u = 0}`, by the same `o(|h|)` estimate, since `|u(x_0+h)|^{3/2} ≤ (L|h|)^{3/2} = o(|h|)`). Here `∇|u| := (u_i ∇u_i)/|u|` off `{u=0}` and `:= 0` on it; `|u|` is Lipschitz and `∇|u| = 0` a.e. on `{u = 0}`, so the identity `u_i ∂_j u_i = |u| ∂_j |u|` holds a.e. All the integrands below are continuous and decay rapidly, so every integral converges and every integration by parts on `R^3` has no boundary term.

(2) *Transport term.* `∫ |u| u_i (u_j ∂_j u_i) dx = ∫ u_j ∂_j (|u|^3/3) dx = −(1/3) ∫ (div u) |u|^3 dx = 0`, using step (1) for `∂_j |u|^3 = 3|u| u_i∂_j u_i` and `div u = 0`. This step uses only the pointwise transport structure of the nonlinearity; it is the step that fails for the averaged equation (T1 below).

(3) *Viscous term.* `∫ |u| u_i Δu_i = −∫ ∂_j(|u| u_i) ∂_j u_i = −∫ |u| |∇u|^2 − ∫ u_i (∂_j |u|) ∂_j u_i = −∫ |u| |∇u|^2 − ∫ |u| |∇|u||^2`, by (1).

(4) *Pressure term.* `−∫ |u| u·∇p = ∫ p div(|u| u) = ∫ p (|u| div u + u·∇|u|) = ∫ p (u·∇|u|)`.

Multiplying `∂_t u = νΔu − (u·∇)u − ∇p` by `3|u| u` and integrating gives the claim. The `|u|^{3/2}` form is `∫ |∇w|^2 = (9/4) ∫ |u| |∇|u||^2`, so `3ν ∫ |u||∇|u||^2 = (4ν/3) ∫ |∇w|^2`. ∎

The identity is verified independently in Part 2 of the script (spectral, on `T^3`, where every step of the proof is the same): the direct `3 ∫ |u| u·∂_t u` and `−νD + P` agree, and the residual falls with the grid as the quadrature of the non-band-limited `|u|` improves. See D4.

### D2. Small-data global regularity in `L^3` with an explicit threshold — **PROVED-HERE**, modulo two cited constants

Two constants are needed, both stated on `R^3`.

- **(C1) Pressure.** `‖p‖_{L^3} ≤ C_p ‖ |u|^2 ‖_{L^3} = C_p ‖u‖_{L^6}^2` with `C_p ≤ 3^{8/3} = 18.720754`. *Proof:* `‖p‖_3 ≤ Σ_{i,j} ‖R_i R_j (u_i u_j)‖_3 ≤ 3 Σ_{i,j} ‖u_i u_j‖_3` using `‖R_j‖_{L^3 → L^3} = cot(π/6) = √3`; then `‖u_i u_j‖_3 ≤ ‖u_i‖_6 ‖u_j‖_6` (Cauchy-Schwarz in `L^3`), so `Σ_{i,j} ‖u_iu_j‖_3 ≤ (Σ_i ‖u_i‖_6)^2 ≤ 3 Σ_i ‖u_i‖_6^2`; and with `a_i = ∫|u_i|^6`, concavity of `t ↦ t^{1/3}` gives `Σ_i a_i^{1/3} ≤ 3^{2/3} (Σ_i a_i)^{1/3} ≤ 3^{2/3} ‖u‖_6^2`. Total `3 · 3 · 3^{2/3} = 3^{8/3}`. The only non-elementary input is the sharp single-Riesz-transform norm `cot(π/(2p*))`, `p* = max(p,p')` — Iwaniec-Martin, extending Pichorides' Hilbert-transform constant by the method of rotations \[checked via WebSearch: the attribution and the value `cot(π/(2p*))` for individual Riesz transforms; the exact reference and the *vector* Riesz norm, which is a different and still open problem, are not used\]. **The summation is very lossy**: D4 gives the de Leeuw lower bound `C_p ≥ 0.398`, so the truth is somewhere in `[0.398, 18.72]`, and I make no claim about where.
- **(C2) Gagliardo-Nirenberg.** `‖w‖_{L^4(R^3)} ≤ C_GN ‖∇w‖_{L^2}^{3/4} ‖w‖_{L^2}^{1/4}`. Two values are used: the **rigorous** `C_GN ≤ C_S^{3/4} = 0.5284690904`, from Hölder `‖w‖_4 ≤ ‖w‖_2^{1/4} ‖w‖_6^{3/4}` and Talenti's sharp Sobolev constant `C_S = 0.4272605429` (closed form, `C_S^{-2} = 3(π/2)^{4/3}`, evaluated in Part 1); and the **sharp** `C_GN = 4^{1/4} 3^{-3/8} M^{-1/4} = 0.4492570155` with `M = ‖Q‖_{L^2}^2 = 18.897251` for the ground state of `−ΔQ + Q = Q^3` on `R^3` (Weinstein's variational characterization, KNOWN [unverified]), which is **COMPUTED** here by radial shooting and cross-checked against the two Pohozaev identities `‖∇Q‖_2^2 = 3‖Q‖_2^2` and `‖Q‖_4^4 = 4‖Q‖_2^2` to `10^{-12}` (Part 1). The sharp value is a computed number, not a closed form, so results using it are flagged.

**Theorem D2.** Let `u^0` be smooth, divergence-free and Schwartz on `R^3`, and let `u` be the unique maximal smooth solution with `f ≡ 0`. Put

```text
κ := 4ν / (3 C_p C_GN^{4/3}).
```

If `‖u^0‖_{L^3(R^3)} < κ` then `T* = ∞`: the solution is global and smooth, and `t ↦ ‖u(t)‖_{L^3}` is nonincreasing. With the rigorous constants, `κ = 0.166695 ν`; with the sharp `C_GN` (and the same `C_p`), `κ = 0.206993 ν` (Part 1).

*Proof.* Write `Y = ‖u‖_3^3`, `w = |u|^{3/2}`, `X = ‖∇w‖_2`, `θ := 2 C_p C_GN^{4/3}`, so `κ = (8ν/3)/θ`.

(i) Since `∇w = (3/2)|u|^{1/2} ∇|u|`, we have `|u| |∇|u|| = (2/3) |u|^{1/2} |∇w|`, hence `|P(u)| ≤ 3 ∫ |p| |u| |∇|u|| = 2 ∫ |p| |u|^{1/2} |∇w| ≤ 2 (∫ |p|^2 |u|)^{1/2} X` by Cauchy-Schwarz.

(ii) Hölder with exponents `(3/2, 3)`: `∫ |p|^2 |u| ≤ ‖p‖_3^2 ‖u‖_3 = ‖p‖_3^2 Y^{1/3}`. So `|P(u)| ≤ 2 ‖p‖_3 Y^{1/6} X`.

(iii) By (C1) and (C2), `‖p‖_3 ≤ C_p ‖u‖_6^2 = C_p ‖w‖_4^{4/3} ≤ C_p C_GN^{4/3} ‖∇w‖_2 ‖w‖_2^{1/3} = C_p C_GN^{4/3} X Y^{1/6}` (using `‖w‖_2^2 = Y`). Hence

```text
|P(u)| ≤ 2 C_p C_GN^{4/3} Y^{1/3} X^2 = θ Y^{1/3} X^2 = θ ‖u‖_{L^3} X^2.
```

(iv) With D1 and `D(u) ≥ (8/3)X^2`: `dY/dt ≤ −(8ν/3) X^2 + θ Y^{1/3} X^2 = (θ ‖u(t)‖_3 − 8ν/3) X^2`.

(v) Let `N_0 = ‖u^0‖_3 < κ` and `δ := 8ν/3 − θ N_0 > 0`. Let `T_1 = sup{ t < T* : ‖u(s)‖_3 ≤ N_0 for all s ≤ t }`; `T_1 > 0` by continuity. On `[0,T_1)` step (iv) gives `dY/dt ≤ −δ X^2 ≤ 0`, so `Y` is nonincreasing there and `‖u(t)‖_3 ≤ N_0`, which by continuity forces `T_1 = T*`. Hence `‖u(t)‖_3 ≤ N_0` on `[0,T*)` and `∫_0^{T*} X(t)^2 dt ≤ Y(0)/δ = N_0^3/δ`.

(vi) By (C2) again, `‖u‖_6^4 = ‖w‖_4^{8/3} ≤ C_GN^{8/3} X^2 Y^{1/3}`, so `∫_0^{T*} ‖u(t)‖_{L^6}^4 dt ≤ C_GN^{8/3} N_0 · N_0^3/δ < ∞` uniformly in `T*`. Thus `u ∈ L^4_t L^6_x(0,T*)`, and `2/4 + 3/6 = 1`: this is a Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin class (`00_problem.md` item 2.5), so if `T* < ∞` the solution extends past `T*`, contradicting maximality. Hence `T* = ∞`. (Alternatively invoke ESS, item 2.4: `sup_t ‖u(t)‖_3 ≤ N_0 < ∞` already forces regularity; but LPS is far more elementary and suffices.) ∎

**What is and is not sharp.** `C_S` is sharp and in closed form; the sharp `C_GN` is a computed number; `C_p ≤ 3^{8/3}` is a crude summation over nine terms on top of a sharp scalar constant and is the dominant loss; steps (i) and (ii) each apply a Cauchy-Schwarz/Hölder that is far from equality (in particular `|u·∇|u||` is replaced by `|u||∇|u||`, discarding the angle between `u` and `∇|u|`). D4 measures the total loss: `κ` is at least a factor `4·10^2` below the sharp threshold on the fields probed. **This theorem is not new**: global regularity for small data in `L^3` is Kato 1984 (`00_problem.md` item 2.15, KNOWN). What is produced here is an explicit number and an exact accounting of where the number is lost.

**On `T^3`.** The statement is made on `R^3` only. On the torus `w = |u|^{3/2}` is not mean-zero, the Sobolev inequality acquires an additive `‖w‖_{L^2}` term, and the closure ceases to be scale-invariant — the box size enters and the resulting threshold is no longer a pure multiple of `ν`. The `L^3` identity D1 itself holds verbatim on `T^3` (this is what the script checks); only the closure D2 does not.

### D3. Why the argument cannot close for large data — **PROVED-HERE**

Define, for smooth Schwartz divergence-free `u ≢ 0` on `R^3`,

```text
K(u) := P(u) / ( X(u)^2 ‖u‖_{L^3} ),      K* := sup_u K(u),
Θ(u) := ν D(u) ‖u‖_{L^3} / P(u)   (when P(u) > 0),      κ_sharp := inf_u Θ(u).
```

**Proposition D3.** (a) `K` and `Θ` are invariant both under the Navier-Stokes scaling `u ↦ λ u(λ ·)` and under the amplitude scaling `u ↦ a u`, `a > 0`. (b) Step (iii) of D2 says exactly `K* ≤ θ = 2 C_p C_GN^{4/3}`, and D2 proves global regularity precisely on the scale-invariant set `{‖u^0‖_3 < (8ν/3)/θ}`. (c) `κ_sharp` is the exact threshold of the method: `d/dt ‖u‖_3^3 |_{t=0} ≤ 0` for every field of `L^3` norm below `κ_sharp`, and there is a field of `L^3` norm `κ_sharp` (or of norm arbitrarily close to it) at which it is `≥ 0`. (d) `(8ν/3)/K* ≤ κ_sharp`, and `κ_sharp > 0`. (e) No rescaling, covering, or induction-on-scales argument enlarges the set in (b).

*Proof.* (a) From the scalings recorded in "Setting and notation": under `u ↦ u_λ` all of `Y`, `‖u‖_3` are invariant while `X^2`, `D`, `P` all carry the same factor `λ^2`, so the ratios are invariant; under `u ↦ au` we have `D(au) = a^3 D(u)`, `X(au)^2 = a^3 X(u)^2`, `‖au‖_3 = a ‖u‖_3` and `P(au) = a^4 P(u)`, so `K(au) = a^4/(a^3 · a) · K(u) = K(u)`, and `Θ(au) = a^3 · a/a^4 · Θ(u) = Θ(u)`. (b) is the definition of `θ` and step (iv)-(v) of D2. (c) is the definition of `Θ`: for a fixed field `v` and amplitude `a`, `d/dt ‖av‖_3^3 = −a^3 ν D(v) + a^4 P(v)`, which changes sign exactly at `a = ν D(v)/P(v)`, i.e. at `L^3` norm `Θ(v)`. (d) `Θ(u) = ν D(u)‖u‖_3/P(u) ≥ ν (8/3) X^2 ‖u‖_3 / (K(u) X^2 ‖u‖_3) = (8ν/3)/K(u)`; positivity of `κ_sharp` is (b) with the finite `θ`. (e) Suppose an argument covered the data set by rescaled copies. By (a) every quantity entering (iv) is scale-invariant, so the differential inequality at scale `λ` is the *same* inequality; a field with `‖u^0‖_3 ≥ κ` has `‖u^0_λ‖_3 ≥ κ` for every `λ`. There is no `λ` at which the bracket `θ‖u‖_3 − 8ν/3` acquires a sign it did not have. ∎

This is the precise form of the supercriticality barrier (`00_problem.md` item 2.1) for this particular functional: the dissipation `−(8ν/3)X^2` and the pressure work `θ‖u‖_3 X^2` have *identical* homogeneity in both scalings, so the dissipation buys nothing at large data. The only conceivable escape inside this framework is to show that the pressure work is systematically smaller than its Hölder bound — i.e. `K* ≤ 0`, which would prove global regularity outright. **`K* ≤ 0` is false**: D3b exhibits (and D4 computes) fields with `P(u) > 0`. So `κ_sharp < ∞` and the `L^3` monotonicity method has a genuine, finite ceiling. The correct reading of D2 through the T6 lens (`00_problem.md` §3): D2 *does* produce an LPS bound, which T6 flags as suspicious; the flag is discharged by the observation that D2 assumes smallness and reproves a known theorem (Kato 1984), and by D3, which shows the argument cannot be pushed past `κ_sharp`.

### D3b. A class where the pressure work vanishes at any amplitude — **PROVED-HERE** and **COMPUTED**

**Proposition D3b.** Let `u` be smooth and divergence-free on `T^3 = (R/2πZ)^3` and *antiperiodic*: there is `a ∈ R^3` with `u(x + a) = −u(x)` for all `x`. Then `P(u) = 0`, so `d/dt ‖u‖_{L^3}^3 = −ν D(u) ≤ 0` at that instant, **for every amplitude**. In particular this holds for every divergence-free field whose Fourier support lies in the odd sublattice `{k : k_1 + k_2 + k_3 odd}` — e.g. the whole shell `|k| = 1` (the twelve real modes `cos x_j`, `sin x_j` with polarizations orthogonal to `e_j`), which contains the ABC flows.

*Proof.* Let `τ` denote translation by `a`. From `u ∘ τ = −u` we get `|u| ∘ τ = |u|` and, since `p = R_iR_j(u_iu_j)` is quadratic in `u` and translation-invariant, `p ∘ τ = p`. Hence `(u·∇|u|) ∘ τ = −(u·∇|u|)`, so `f := p (u·∇|u|)` satisfies `f ∘ τ = −f`. Translation preserves Lebesgue measure on `T^3`, so `∫ f = ∫ f ∘ τ = −∫ f`, i.e. `∫ f = 0` and `P(u) = 3∫f = 0`. For a field supported in the odd sublattice take `a = π(1,1,1)`: `e^{i k·a} = e^{iπ(k_1+k_2+k_3)} = −1` on that sublattice. ∎

**What this does and does not say.** It says the threshold of D2 is very far from being pointwise sharp: there are fields of arbitrarily large `L^3` norm at which `‖u‖_{L^3}` is instantaneously decreasing. It does **not** give any regularity, because the antiperiodicity class is not invariant under the flow: the Navier-Stokes nonlinearity is quadratic, hence *even* under `u ↦ −u`, while `∂_t` and `Δ` are odd, so `u ↦ −u(· + a)` is not a symmetry of the equation and the parity is destroyed immediately. This is the same phenomenon Line A records in a different guise (a functional can decrease at a state without being a Lyapunov functional). It is also the reason the numerical search of D4 finds nothing on the `|k| = 1` shell and must mix `|k|^2 = 1` with `|k|^2 = 2` to see the pressure work at all.

### D5. The Type I reduction to a Liouville problem — **KNOWN**

Say a solution has a **Type I** singularity at `(x_0, T*)` if `|u(x,t)| ≤ C (T* − t)^{-1/2}` near it, and **Type II** otherwise. Leray's lower bound `‖u(t)‖_∞ ≥ c(T*−t)^{-1/2}` (`00_problem.md` item 2.5) says Type I is the *slowest possible* rate, so the dichotomy is exhaustive: a blowup is Type I (rate exactly `(T*−t)^{-1/2}`) or Type II (strictly faster).

**Reduction (KNOWN).** Suppose `u` has a Type I singularity at `(x_0,T*)`. Take `λ_k ↓ 0` and set `U_k(y,s) := λ_k u(x_0 + λ_k y, T* + λ_k^2 s)`, which solves the same equations on `R^3 × (−(T* )/λ_k^2, 0)` with the same `ν`. The Type I bound gives `|U_k(y,s)| ≤ C |s|^{-1/2}` uniformly in `k`, so on each parabolic cylinder `{|s| ≥ ε}` the family is uniformly bounded; interior estimates for Navier-Stokes (Serrin/CKN local regularity from an `L^∞` bound) give uniform bounds on all derivatives there, and a diagonal extraction produces a limit `U` which is a bounded mild ancient solution on `R^3 × (−∞,0)`. An `ε`-regularity argument (`00_problem.md` item 2.6) shows `U ≢ 0` if `(x_0,T*)` was genuinely singular. Hence: **a Liouville theorem for bounded mild ancient solutions excludes Type I blowup.** This is the Seregin-Šverák programme; the ancient-solution formulation and the two solved cases are Koch-Nadirashvili-Seregin-Šverák, "Liouville theorems for the Navier-Stokes equations and applications", Acta Math. 203 (2009), 83-105 [checked via WebSearch: authors, title, venue, volume, pages, and the two theorems quoted next].

**Known cases (KNOWN).** (a) In 2D every bounded mild ancient solution is constant. (b) In 3D, every bounded mild ancient axisymmetric solution with zero swirl (`u_θ ≡ 0`) is constant. [Both checked via WebSearch, from the paper's own summary.] (c) With swirl, under the additional scale-invariant bound `|u| ≤ C/r` (`r` the distance to the axis), the corresponding no-Type-I-blowup conclusion is due to Chen-Strain-Tsai-Yau and to KNSS \[unverified; recalled from `00_problem.md` item 2.9\]. The general 3D Liouville problem is **open**, and by 2.2 it must be, since the averaged equation blows up while every argument using only bilinear estimates transfers to it (see D6 and the transfer check).

### D6. Liouville for bounded mild ancient solutions small in `L^∞_s L^3_x` — **PROVED-HERE**

Normalize `ν = 1`: if `u` solves the `ν`-equation then `w(x,s) = u(x, s/ν)/ν` solves the `ν = 1` equation, `‖w(s)‖_{L^3} = ‖u(s/ν)‖_{L^3}/ν`, and ancient-ness is preserved. The following is standard-looking (it is the ancient-solution analogue of Kato's small-data uniqueness) and I expect it is known; the proof is written out because it is short, because it is what D7 needs, and because it is the piece that transfers to Tao's averaged equation.

**Theorem D6.** There is an absolute `ε_0 > 0` with the following property. Let `U` be a bounded mild ancient solution of the `ν = 1` Navier-Stokes equations on `R^3 × (−∞,0)`, with `A := sup_{s<0} ‖U(s)‖_{L^∞} < ∞` and `N := sup_{s<0} ‖U(s)‖_{L^3} < ∞`. If `N < ε_0` then `U ≡ 0`. Explicitly `ε_0 = 1/(8 (C_2 C_6)^{1/2})`, where `C_q` is the constant in the Oseen estimate (O) below. For general `ν`, the hypothesis reads `N < ε_0 ν`.

*Input.* **(O)** `‖e^{tΔ} P div F‖_{L^∞(R^3)} ≤ C_q t^{-1/2 − 3/(2q)} ‖F‖_{L^q(R^3)}` for `1 ≤ q ≤ ∞`, `t > 0`. This is the standard Oseen-kernel estimate: the kernel of `e^{tΔ} P div` is `t^{-2} G(x/√t)` with `G` smooth and rapidly decaying, so its `L^{q'}` norm is `C_q t^{-2 + 3/(2q')} = C_q t^{-1/2 − 3/(2q)}`; KNOWN [unverified as to a reference; Solonnikov/Oseen]. The constants `C_q` are not evaluated here, so `ε_0` is explicit only in terms of them.

*Proof.* Fix `s < 0`. For `s_0 < s`, mildness gives `U(s) = e^{(s−s_0)Δ} U(s_0) − ∫_{s_0}^{s} e^{(s−τ)Δ} P div(U ⊗ U) dτ`. For the linear part, `‖e^{tΔ} g‖_{L^∞} ≤ ‖G_t‖_{L^{3/2}} ‖g‖_{L^3} = c t^{-1/2} ‖g‖_{L^3}`, so `‖e^{(s−s_0)Δ}U(s_0)‖_∞ ≤ c (s−s_0)^{-1/2} N → 0` as `s_0 → −∞`. The Duhamel integral converges absolutely in `L^∞` by the estimates below, so

```text
U(s) = − ∫_{−∞}^{s} e^{(s−τ)Δ} P div (U ⊗ U)(τ) dτ    in L^∞(R^3).
```

Let `T > 0` be free and split at `τ = s − T`. On the near piece use (O) with `q = 6`: the exponent is `−1/2 − 1/4 = −3/4`, `∫_{s−T}^{s} (s−τ)^{-3/4} dτ = 4 T^{1/4}`, and `‖U ⊗ U‖_{L^6} ≤ ‖U‖_{L^{12}}^2 ≤ (‖U‖_3^{1/4} ‖U‖_∞^{3/4})^2 = N^{1/2} A^{3/2}` by interpolation. On the far piece use `q = 2`: the exponent is `−1/2 − 3/4 = −5/4`, `∫_{−∞}^{s−T} (s−τ)^{-5/4} dτ = 4 T^{-1/4}`, and `‖U ⊗ U‖_{L^2} ≤ ‖U‖_{L^4}^2 ≤ (‖U‖_3^{3/4}‖U‖_∞^{1/4})^2 = N^{3/2} A^{1/2}`. Hence

```text
A ≤ 4 C_6 T^{1/4} N^{1/2} A^{3/2} + 4 C_2 T^{-1/4} N^{3/2} A^{1/2}.
```

Both terms are finite, which also justifies the absolute convergence used above. If `A = 0` we are done; otherwise minimize the right-hand side over `t := T^{1/4} > 0`. The minimum of `αt + βt^{-1}` is `2√(αβ)` with `α = 4C_6 N^{1/2}A^{3/2}`, `β = 4C_2 N^{3/2}A^{1/2}`, giving `2√(16 C_2C_6 N^2 A^2) = 8 (C_2C_6)^{1/2} N A`. So `A ≤ 8 (C_2C_6)^{1/2} N A`, and if `8(C_2C_6)^{1/2} N < 1` this forces `A = 0`, i.e. `U ≡ 0`. ∎

Two remarks. (1) The hypothesis is on a **scale-invariant** quantity (`sup_s ‖U(s)‖_{L^3}` is unchanged by `U ↦ λ U(λ·, λ^2 ·)`), so unlike a bound on `‖U‖_∞` it cannot be normalized away — see D8(a). (2) Removing the smallness is exactly the content of ESS-type backward uniqueness, and is the breakpoint of this line: I have no argument for large `N`.

### D7. Liouville under a small critical vorticity bound — **PROVED-HERE** under two stated hypotheses

`sup_s ‖Ω(s)‖_{L^{3/2}}` is scale-invariant: `Ω_λ(y,s) = λ^2 Ω(λy, λ^2 s)` has `‖Ω_λ(s)‖_{3/2} = λ^2 λ^{-2} ‖Ω‖_{3/2}`. So this is a genuine hypothesis in the sense of D8(a).

**Theorem D7.** Let `U` be a bounded mild ancient solution of the `ν = 1` equations on `R^3 × (−∞,0)`, `A = sup|U| < ∞`, `Ω = curl U`, with

- **(i)** `M := sup_{s<0} ‖Ω(s)‖_{L^{3/2}(R^3)} < ε_0 / C_{BS}`, where `ε_0` is from D6 and `C_{BS}` is the Biot-Savart/Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev constant in `‖K * Ω‖_{L^3} ≤ C_{BS} ‖Ω‖_{L^{3/2}}` (KNOWN);
- **(ii)** there is a pressure `p` making `(U,p)` a classical solution, and a bounded measurable `g : (−∞,0) → R^3`, with `R := sup_{s<0} ‖∇p(·,s) − g(s)‖_{L^{3/2} + L^3} < ∞`.

Then `Ω ≡ 0` and `U(x,s) = b(s)` is independent of `x` — the trivial form in the Koch-Nadirashvili-Seregin-Šverák classification, with `b' = −g`. If moreover `g ≡ 0` then `U` is a constant vector.

Hypothesis (ii) is the natural normalization for a bounded ancient solution, not an ad hoc device: a spatially constant pressure gradient is exactly what the trivial solutions `u = b(t)`, `p = −b'(t)·x` carry (`00_problem.md` §1.2), and (ii) determines `∇p` modulo that ambiguity, because the difference of two admissible `∇p − g` is harmonic and lies in `L^{3/2}+L^3`, hence vanishes by the mean-value argument in step (4).

*Proof.* (1) *Biot-Savart.* `U` is smooth with all derivatives bounded (KNOWN for the mild class), so `Ω ∈ L^{3/2} ∩ L^∞`, hence `V(·,s) := K * Ω(·,s)` (with `K(x) = −(1/4π) x/|x|^3 ×`) is well defined, smooth, in `L^3` with `‖V(s)‖_3 ≤ C_{BS} M`, and bounded (split the kernel at `|x| = 1` and use `Ω ∈ L^{3/2}` far, `Ω ∈ L^∞` near). Since `div Ω = 0`, `curl V = Ω` and `div V = 0`. Then `h := U − V` is curl-free and divergence-free, so each component is harmonic; it is bounded, so by Liouville `h = c(s)` for a vector `c(s)` with `|c(s)| ≤ A + ‖V(s)‖_∞`, smooth in `s`.

(2) *Galilean normalization.* Let `h(s)` solve `h'(s) = c(s)`, `h(−1) = 0`, and set `Ũ(x,s) := U(x + h(s), s) − c(s)`. A direct computation gives `∂_s Ũ + (Ũ·∇)Ũ = Δ Ũ − ∇ p̃` with `p̃(x,s) = p(x + h(s), s) + c'(s)·x`, and `div Ũ = 0`. Also `Ũ(·,s) = V(· + h(s), s)`, so `‖Ũ(s)‖_{L^3} ≤ C_{BS} M` and `‖Ũ‖_∞ ≤ 2A + sup‖V‖_∞ < ∞`.

(3) *The leftover spatially constant force.* Write `∇p = g(s) + ρ(·,s)` with `sup_s ‖ρ(s)‖_{L^{3/2}+L^3} = R < ∞`, so that `∇p̃ = g(s) + c'(s) + ρ(· + h(s), s)`. `Ũ` is smooth on `[s_0,s]`, and the difference between `Ũ` and the right-hand side below solves the heat equation with zero data and lies in `L^∞ + L^{3/2} + L^3` uniformly on `[s_0,s]`, hence has at most Gaussian growth; Tychonov uniqueness applies in that class, so

```text
Ũ(s) = e^{(s−s_0)Δ} Ũ(s_0) − ∫_{s_0}^{s} e^{(s−τ)Δ} [ div(Ũ⊗Ũ) + ρ(· + h(τ), τ) ] dτ − ∫_{s_0}^{s} ( g(τ) + c'(τ) ) dτ,
```

where the last term is spatially constant because constants are fixed by the heat semigroup. Every other term lies in `L^3 + L^{3/2}(R^3)`: `Ũ(s) ∈ L^3`; `e^{tΔ}Ũ(s_0) ∈ L^3`; `∫ ‖e^{(s−τ)Δ} div(Ũ⊗Ũ)‖_{L^3} dτ ≤ c ∫_{s_0}^s (s−τ)^{-1/2} ‖Ũ⊗Ũ‖_{L^3} dτ ≤ 2c (s−s_0)^{1/2} ‖Ũ‖_∞ C_{BS}M < ∞`; and `∫ ‖e^{(s−τ)Δ} ρ(·+h,τ)‖_{L^{3/2}+L^3} dτ ≤ (s−s_0) R < ∞` by (ii), the heat semigroup being a contraction on each `L^q`. Hence the constant vector `∫_{s_0}^s (g + c')` belongs to `L^3 + L^{3/2}(R^3)`, which contains no nonzero constant, for every `s_0 < s`. So `c'(s) = −g(s)` for a.e. `s`, and therefore `∇p̃ = ρ(· + h(s), s)`, which satisfies (ii) with `g = 0`.

(4) *`Ũ` is mild.* Let `q := R_iR_j(Ũ_iŨ_j)`; then `div(Ũ⊗Ũ) + ∇q = P div(Ũ⊗Ũ)` and `Δ(p̃ − q) = 0`. Moreover `∇q ∈ L^3`, since `∂_k(Ũ_iŨ_j) ∈ L^3` (as `Ũ ∈ L^3` and `∇Ũ ∈ L^∞`) and `R_iR_j` is bounded on `L^3`. So by step (3), `∇(p̃−q)` is a harmonic vector field in `L^{3/2}+L^3`; writing it as `g_1 + g_2` and using the mean value property, `|∇(p̃−q)(x)| = |avg_{B_R(x)}| ≤ cR^{-2}‖g_1‖_{3/2} + cR^{-1}‖g_2‖_3 → 0` as `R → ∞`, so `∇(p̃−q) ≡ 0`. Substituting into the Duhamel formula of (3) turns it into the mild formulation for `Ũ`, and the linear term tends to `0` in `L^∞` as `s_0 → −∞` as in D6.

(5) *Conclusion.* `Ũ` is a bounded mild ancient solution with `sup_s ‖Ũ(s)‖_{L^3} ≤ C_{BS} M < ε_0`, so D6 gives `Ũ ≡ 0`, i.e. `V ≡ 0` and `U(x,s) = c(s) =: b(s)`, with `b' = −g` by step (3). Then `Ω = curl U ≡ 0`. ∎

**Honest placement.** D7 is *strictly weaker than ESS* as a blowup exclusion: `‖u‖_{L^3} ≤ C_{BS} ‖ω‖_{L^{3/2}}` (modulo constants) by the same Biot-Savart estimate, so a solution with `sup_t ‖ω(t)‖_{L^{3/2}} < ∞` already has `sup_t ‖u(t)‖_{L^3} < ∞` and is regular by ESS (item 2.4) — no smallness needed and no Type I hypothesis needed. What D7 adds is: (a) a complete, elementary proof (heat semigroup, Hölder, Liouville for harmonic functions — no Carleman inequality, no backward uniqueness, no partial regularity), (b) the identification of the exact obstruction to removing the smallness, and (c) an argument whose engine (D6) transfers to the averaged equation, which ESS does not (see the transfer check). Hypothesis (ii) is a normalization of the pressure at spatial infinity, of exactly the kind `00_problem.md` §1.2 and §1.5 flag as part of the specification; whether the ancient solutions produced by the D5 rescaling satisfy (ii) in this quantitative form is **not verified here** and is recorded in the Breakpoint.

### D8. What "smallness" can mean for ancient solutions; and a FAILED attempt

**(a) Scaling triviality of non-invariant smallness — PROVED-HERE.** The class `A` of bounded mild ancient solutions is invariant under `S_λ : U ↦ λ U(λ ·, λ^2 ·)` for every `λ > 0`, and `S_λ` is a bijection of `A` with inverse `S_{1/λ}`. Let `q : A → [0,∞]` be any functional with `q(S_λ U) = λ^a q(U)` for some `a > 0`. Then for every `ε > 0` the statement "`U ∈ A` and `q(U) < ε` imply `U` trivial" is **equivalent** to the unrestricted Liouville statement for `A`: given any `U ∈ A` with `q(U) < ∞`, choose `λ` with `λ^a q(U) < ε`, apply the hypothesis to `S_λ U`, and pull back. *Consequences:* (i) `q = ‖U‖_{L^∞}` has `a = 1`, so **no `L^∞`-smallness hypothesis carries any information**; (ii) `q = ` the Lipschitz constant of the vorticity direction `ξ = Ω/|Ω|` in `x` has `a = 1` (since `ξ_λ(y,s) = ξ(λy, λ^2 s)`), so in any Liouville theorem under a Constantin-Fefferman-type hypothesis **the Lipschitz constant may be assumed as small as one likes** — the content of the hypothesis is the qualitative statement that `ξ` is Lipschitz at all, not the size of the constant. By contrast `sup_s‖U(s)‖_{L^3}` (D6), `sup_s‖Ω(s)‖_{L^{3/2}}` (D7), and `sup_{r,z} r^{-2}∬_{Q_r(z)}(|U|^3+|P|^{3/2})` are scale-invariant (`a = 0`), so smallness in them is a genuine restriction.

**(b) FAILED: a Liouville theorem under a Lipschitz vorticity direction.** *Attempt.* Let `U ∈ A` with `ξ = Ω/|Ω|` Lipschitz in `x` uniformly in `s` where `Ω ≠ 0`; by (a)(ii) assume the constant `L` is as small as desired. Constantin-Fefferman's geometric depletion (`00_problem.md` item 2.7 refinements [unverified]) bounds the vortex-stretching factor `α = ξ·(∇U)ξ` by a Biot-Savart integral whose kernel carries a factor `|sin θ(x,y)|` measuring the misalignment of `ξ(x)` and `ξ(y)`; with `ξ` Lipschitz, `|sinθ| ≤ L|x−y|`, so `|α(x)| ≲ L ∫ |x−y|^{-2} |Ω(y)| dy = L · I_1(|Ω|)(x)`. Feeding this into `d/ds ∫ |Ω|^2 = 2∫ α |Ω|^2 − 2∫|∇Ω|^2` and using Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev `‖I_1 f‖_{L^3} ≤ c‖f‖_{L^{3/2}}` gives `∫ α|Ω|^2 ≤ ‖I_1|Ω|‖_3 ‖Ω‖_{L^3}^2 ≲ L ‖Ω‖_{3/2} ‖Ω‖_3^2`, and one would then absorb `‖Ω‖_3^2` into the dissipation and take `L` small.

*Where it breaks — the exact step.* The very first display after the depletion bound. `∫ α |Ω|^2 ≤ ‖I_1|Ω|‖_{L^3}‖Ω‖_{L^3}^2` requires `Ω(·,s) ∈ L^{3/2} ∩ L^3`, and a bounded ancient solution has **no decay at all** — `Ω` is merely in `L^∞`, and `∫|Ω|^2` is typically infinite, so the global enstrophy inequality that the argument differentiates does not exist. The natural repair is to localize: replace `∫|Ω|^2` by `∫ χ_R^2 |Ω|^2` with `χ_R(x) = χ(x/R)`. This fails for a reason that is structural, not technical: every term in the localized inequality is scale-invariant in the same way. The commutator terms produced by the cutoff, `∫ |Ω|^2 (U·∇)χ_R^2` and `∫ |Ω|^2 |∇χ_R|^2`, carry `R^{-1}` and `R^{-2}` against a main term that also carries `R^{-1}`-type weights after the ancient-solution rescaling `S_R`, so passing `R → ∞` does not make them subordinate; and the Riesz potential `I_1(χ_R|Ω|)` differs from `χ_R I_1(|Ω|)` by a tail that is of the same order as the main term because `|Ω|` does not decay. In short: the Constantin-Fefferman method is a *finite-energy* method, and the ancient-solution class is exactly the class where finite energy has been scaled away. Status **FAILED**; no partial result is claimed. (For the record, D8(a)(ii) is not affected: it is a statement about hypotheses, not about this proof.)

### D9. Is Tao's averaged blowup Type I or Type II? — **CONJECTURE**

Tao's averaged equation (`00_problem.md` item 2.2) obeys the same energy identity and the same scaling, so Leray's lower bound argument transfers verbatim (it uses only local well-posedness in a subcritical norm plus scaling), giving `‖u(t)‖_{L^∞} ≥ c(T*−t)^{-1/2}`: the blowup is **at least** Type I in rate. Which of the two it is depends on how much energy the cascade keeps at the active scale, and I could not verify this against the paper in this environment. Two readings:

- *Energy-preserving cascade (my reading of the "self-replicating machine").* If a fixed fraction of the energy `E` sits at scale `L_n ~ 2^{-n}` at stage `n`, then the amplitude is `A_n ~ (E/L_n^3)^{1/2} ~ 2^{3n/2}`, the eddy-turnover time of stage `n` is `T_n ~ L_n/A_n ~ 2^{-5n/2}` (summable, hence finite-time blowup), and `T* − t ~ 2^{-5n/2}` gives `‖u(t)‖_∞ ~ A_n ~ (T*−t)^{-3/5}`. Since `3/5 > 1/2`, this is **Type II**.
- *Exactly discretely self-similar blowup.* Then `‖u(t)‖_∞ ~ (T*−t)^{-1/2}` by definition, i.e. **Type I**, and the energy in the singular region must vanish like `(T*−t)^{1/2}` — the cascade would be losing essentially all of its energy at each stage, which is not how a "self-replicating machine" is described.

I mark the `3/5` computation **CONJECTURE** [unverified: the exponent is reconstructed from the scaling of an energy-preserving cascade, not read off Tao's theorem]. The consequence, either way, is recorded in the transfer check: if Tao's blowup is Type II, then the D5 reduction has nothing to say about it, which is consistent — the Liouville route is a Type I route only.

## Breakpoint

This line stops in three distinct places; each is a precisely stated open step, not a technical gap.

**B1 (the `L^3` wall, D2/D3).** The estimate closes only for `‖u^0‖_{L^3} < κ_sharp`, and `κ_sharp < ∞` because `K* > 0` (D3d, exhibited in D4e). The exact breaking step is step (iv) of D2: the bracket `θ‖u(t)‖_{L^3} − 8ν/3` multiplying `X^2`. Both terms are of the same homogeneity under both the parabolic scaling and the amplitude scaling (D3a), so the dissipation cannot dominate the pressure work at large data, at any scale, in any covering or induction. To go further one must show that the pressure work `P(u) = 3∫p(u·∇|u|)` is *systematically* smaller along solutions than its Hölder bound allows — i.e. use the dynamics, not a pointwise inequality on fields. Nothing in this line does that. The quantitative distance to the truth is unknown: D4 brackets `κ_sharp` only between `0.167ν` and `152ν`.

**B2 (the ancient Liouville problem, D6/D7).** Both theorems need smallness of a scale-invariant quantity, and there is no mechanism here to remove it. The obstruction is visible in the proof of D6: the fixed-point inequality `A ≤ 8(C_2C_6)^{1/2} N A` is a *perturbative* statement — it is the ancient-solution form of Kato's small-data theory, and it uses nothing but bilinear estimates and the heat semigroup. Removing the smallness is, by item 2.4, essentially the content of ESS (blowup/rescaling plus backward uniqueness via Carleman inequalities), and by T1 it *cannot* be done with the tools used here, because the same tools apply to the averaged equation, which has a nontrivial blowup. A secondary, smaller gap: hypothesis (ii) of D7 (a pressure normalization at spatial infinity) is assumed, not verified for the ancient solutions produced by the D5 rescaling.

**B3 (the reduction itself, D5/D9).** Even a complete Liouville theorem for bounded mild ancient solutions would only exclude **Type I** blowup. Type II is untouched, and D9 suggests (CONJECTURE) that the one blowup we actually possess in a Navier-Stokes-like system — Tao's averaged equation — is Type II. So the Liouville route, even if it succeeded, would not close (A)/(B); it would reduce the problem to Type II blowup, about which this line says nothing.

## What would be needed next

- **Dynamical depletion of the pressure work.** The single most useful object would be a bound of the form `∫_0^T P(u(t)) dt ≤ (1−η) (8ν/3) ∫_0^T X(t)^2 dt + (controlled error)` valid along solutions with `‖u^0‖_3` large, i.e. a statement true on trajectories but false on fields (D4e shows the pointwise statement is false). By T1 the proof of any such bound must use the exact nonlinearity — the natural candidate being the transport structure that already made D1 step (2) vanish, applied to `p` itself through `Dp/Dt`.
- **The sharp constants.** `C_p` (the `L^3` norm of `F ↦ R_iR_jF_{ij}` on symmetric matrix fields; equivalently, after removing the trace, a Beurling-Ahlfors-type operator in 3D) is bracketed here only in `[0.398, 18.72]`. Determining it, or even improving the upper bound below `3`, would move `κ` by an order of magnitude; the traceless decomposition `p = −|u|^2/3 + R_iR_jD_{ij}` with `|D| = √(2/3)|u|^2` is the obvious starting point and was not pushed here.
- **A better probe of `κ_sharp`.** The optimization in D4d is over 52 low-frequency periodic modes and almost certainly misses the maximizer of `K`. A search over anisotropic, spatially concentrated profiles on `R^3` (shear layers, vortex tubes with axial variation) with an adjoint-based gradient would tighten the upper bracket enormously and would say whether `κ` is off by `10` or by `10^3`.
- **Hypothesis (ii) of D7.** Show that the ancient solutions produced by the D5 rescaling of a Type I blowup admit a pressure with `sup_s ‖∇p(s) − g(s)‖_{L^{3/2}+L^3} < ∞` for some bounded `g`, or restate D7 in the KNSS mild class without any pressure hypothesis.
- **Type II.** By B3 the Liouville programme is a Type I programme. The corresponding reduction for Type II (which would produce a limit that is not bounded, and thus is not in the KNSS class at all) does not exist and is where the actual difficulty of (A)/(B) lives.
- **Settle D9** by reading Tao 2016 §1 for the blowup rate; a one-line check of whether `‖u(t)‖_{L^∞} (T*−t)^{1/2}` is bounded in his construction decides whether the Type I reduction is even relevant to the one blowup that is known.
