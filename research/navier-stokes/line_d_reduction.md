# Line D — Conditional reductions: the `L^3` wall and the ancient-solution Liouville problem

## Goal

Two reductions of Clay (A)/(B) to sharply stated lemmas, each attempted to the point where it breaks. (1) The **`L^3` wall**: by Escauriaza–Seregin–Šverák (`00_problem.md` item 2.4) any blowup drives `‖u(t)‖_{L^3}` to infinity, so it suffices to bound the critical Lebesgue norm. I compute `d/dt ‖u‖_{L^3}^3` exactly, isolate the dissipation and the pressure work, and close the estimate with an explicit numerical threshold `κ` — then locate exactly the scale-invariance that blocks the same computation at large data. (2) The **ancient-solution Liouville problem**: Seregin–Šverák rescaling converts a Type I blowup into a nontrivial bounded mild ancient solution on `R^3 × (−∞,0)`, so a Liouville theorem for that class excludes Type I blowup. I prove two Liouville theorems, **each under a smallness hypothesis on a scale-invariant norm** (one in `L^∞_s L^3_x`, one in `L^∞_s L^{3/2}_x` for the vorticity), and record where a third attempt — the Constantin–Fefferman vorticity-direction hypothesis — fails.

Nothing here solves or claims to solve the Clay problem. What is produced is: an exact identity; one explicit number `κ ≈ 0.167 ν` with every constant named and every loss located, whose numerical value is **conditional on an unread Riesz-transform constant** (D2 (C1)); a proof that the pressure work is not identically zero, so the method has a genuine finite ceiling **on `T^3`** (D3c); a computed upper bound on the **torus** analogue of the sharp threshold, which does *not* bracket `κ`, because no transference between `T^3` and `R^3` is proved anywhere here (D4d); an explicit `T^3` velocity field at which `‖u‖_{L^3}` increases; two Liouville theorems, **both under smallness of a scale-invariant quantity** (one of them also under a pressure normalization); and one documented failure.

**What round 1 of refereeing changed** (`refutations/line_d_r1.md`, fourteen numbered issues — ten major, four minor — none fatal). The `T^3`/`R^3` conflation in the `κ_sharp` "bracket" is removed and the two functionals are now kept apart throughout; D2's conclusion `T* = ∞` is re-tagged KNOWN-conditional because it is carried by an unread continuation criterion, not by anything proved here; D3(e) is downgraded from PROVED-HERE to a remark plus a CONJECTURE, since it quantified over all conceivable arguments and is false on its literal reading; the standing function class is restated as an explicit integrability class, because the Schwartz class is not propagated by the flow; D5's rescaling is re-centered so that it actually produces a *bounded* ancient solution; D7's step (3) is reproved without the bogus appeal to Tychonov uniqueness; and the three unverifiable assertions about the internals of Tao's `B̃` are marked CONJECTURE. One thing was *gained* in the repair: D3c below replaces the numerics-only claim `K* > 0` by a proof, on `T^3`, with an exactly evaluated constant.

Write

```text
Y(t) = ∫ |u(x,t)|^3 dx = ‖u(t)‖_{L^3}^3,     w = |u|^{3/2},     X(t) = ‖∇ w(t)‖_{L^2},
D(u) = 3 ∫ |u| |∇u|^2 dx + 3 ∫ |u| |∇|u||^2 dx,     P(u) = 3 ∫ p (u·∇|u|) dx.
```

`X^2 = (9/4) ∫ |u| |∇|u||^2` (chain rule), so `D(u) = 3 ∫ |u||∇u|^2 + (4/3) X^2`. Under the Navier-Stokes scaling `u_λ(x,t) = λ u(λx, λ^2 t)` of `00_problem.md` §1.6: `Y` is invariant, `X_λ = λ X`, `D(u_λ) = λ^2 D(u)`, `P(u_λ) = λ^2 P(u)`. Under the amplitude scaling `u ↦ a u` (a fixed field, not a solution): `D(au) = a^3 D(u)` but `P(au) = a^4 P(u)` — the two are of different homogeneity, which is the whole story of D3. Also `P(−u) = −P(u)` while `D(−u) = D(u)`, since `p` is quadratic (even) and `u·∇|u|` is odd.

For ancient solutions, `s` denotes time on `(−∞,0)`, `U` the solution, `Ω = curl U`. A *bounded mild ancient solution* is a bounded `U` on `R^3 × (−∞,0)` with `U(s) = e^{ν(s−s_0)Δ} U(s_0) − ∫_{s_0}^{s} e^{ν(s−τ)Δ} P div(U ⊗ U) dτ` for all `s_0 < s < 0`, `P` the Leray projector (this is the class of Koch-Nadirashvili-Seregin-Šverák). The frequently used regularity statement — *such `U` are smooth in `x` and `s` with `∇U`, `∂_s U`, `∇^2 U` bounded on `R^3 × (−∞,0)`* — is **KNOWN [unverified]**, attributed to the KNSS paper of D5, whose text was never read; it is load-bearing twice in D7 and is therefore carried there as an explicit hypothesis (iii) rather than as background.

### D1. The exact `L^3` identity — **PROVED-HERE**

**Theorem D1.** For a smooth solution as above, for every `t ∈ (0,T*)`,

```text
d/dt ∫ |u|^3 dx = −3ν ∫ |u| |∇u|^2 dx − 3ν ∫ |u| |∇|u||^2 dx + 3 ∫ p (u·∇|u|) dx
                = −ν D(u) + P(u),
```

and the second dissipation term in the `|u|^{3/2}` form is `3ν ∫ |u| |∇|u||^2 = (4ν/3) ∫ |∇(|u|^{3/2})|^2 = (4ν/3) X^2`. The transport term contributes exactly zero. Consequently `D(u) ≥ (8/3) X^2`, since `|∇u|^2 ≥ |∇|u||^2` pointwise gives `3 ∫|u||∇u|^2 ≥ 3·(4/9) X^2 = (4/3) X^2`.

*Proof.* (1) *Chain rule at the zeros of `u`.* `|u|^3 = (|u|^2)^{3/2}` and `s ↦ s^{3/2}` is `C^1` on `[0,∞)`, so `|u|^3 ∈ C^1` in `(x,t)` with `∂_t |u|^3 = 3|u| u·∂_t u` and `∂_j |u|^3 = 3 |u| u_i ∂_j u_i`, both continuous. Likewise `|u| u_i` is `C^1`: away from `{u = 0}` this is clear, and at a zero `x_0`, `| |u| u_i |(x_0 + h) ≤ L^2 |h|^2 = o(|h|)` with `L` a local Lipschitz constant for `u`, so it is differentiable there with derivative `0`, and `∂_j(|u| u_i) = |u| ∂_j u_i + u_i ∂_j |u|` is continuous because `|u_i ∂_j|u|| ≤ |u| |∇u| → 0`. Finally `w = |u|^{3/2}` is `C^1` with `∇w = (3/2)|u|^{1/2} ∇|u|` (and `= 0` on `{u = 0}`, by the same `o(|h|)` estimate, since `|u(x_0+h)|^{3/2} ≤ (L|h|)^{3/2} = o(|h|)`). Here `∇|u| := (u_i ∇u_i)/|u|` off `{u=0}` and `:= 0` on it; `|u|` is Lipschitz and `∇|u| = 0` a.e. on `{u = 0}`, so the identity `u_i ∂_j u_i = |u| ∂_j |u|` holds a.e. *Convergence and integration by parts, from (S) alone.* The lemma used throughout is: if `F ∈ L^1(R^3)` and `∂_j F ∈ L^1(R^3)` then `∫ ∂_j F = 0`. (Take `χ ∈ C_c^∞`, `χ ≡ 1` on `B_1`, `χ_R(x) = χ(x/R)`; then `∫ (∂_jF) χ_R = −∫ F ∂_j χ_R`, and `|∫ F ∂_j χ_R| ≤ R^{-1}‖∇χ‖_∞ ‖F‖_{L^1} → 0`, while `∫(∂_jF)χ_R → ∫ ∂_j F` by dominated convergence.) The three fluxes are `F_j = u_j |u|^3/3` (step 2), `F_j = |u| u_i ∂_j u_i` (step 3) and `F_j = p |u| u_j` (step 4). Each lies in `L^1` together with its divergence, by Hölder from (S): in step (3), `|u| ∈ L^2`, `u_i ∈ L^2`, `∂_j u_i ∈ L^∞` give `F ∈ L^1`, and `∂_j F = |u||∇u|^2 + u_i (∂_j|u|)(∂_j u_i) + |u| u_i Δu_i ∈ L^1` because `u ∈ L^∞`, `∇u ∈ L^2`, `|u| ∈ L^2`, `∇^2 u ∈ L^2`; in step (2), `u_j|u|^3 ∈ L^1` since `u ∈ L^2 ∩ L^∞ ⊂ L^3`, and `∂_j F = u_j |u| u_i ∂_j u_i ∈ L^1`; in step (4), `p ∈ L^2`, `|u| ∈ L^2`, `u_j ∈ L^∞` give `F ∈ L^1`, and `∂_j F = (∂_j p)|u|u_j + p (u·∇|u|) ∈ L^1` because `∇p ∈ L^2` and `∇|u| ∈ L^2`. The integrals themselves converge for the same reasons: `∫|u|^3 < ∞`, `∫|u||∇u|^2 ≤ ‖u‖_∞ ‖∇u‖_2^2 < ∞`, `∫|p| |u| |∇|u|| ≤ ‖p‖_2 ‖u‖_∞ ‖∇u‖_2 < ∞`. Differentiation under the integral sign in `d/dt ∫|u|^3` is justified by the local-in-`t` uniformity in (S) and dominated convergence. **No rapid decay is used anywhere.** The previous version of this proof discharged all of this with the single sentence "the integrands decay rapidly", which by the Setting is not available for `t > 0`.

(2) *Transport term.* `∫ |u| u_i (u_j ∂_j u_i) dx = ∫ u_j ∂_j (|u|^3/3) dx = −(1/3) ∫ (div u) |u|^3 dx = 0`, using step (1) for `∂_j |u|^3 = 3|u| u_i∂_j u_i` and `div u = 0`. This step uses only the pointwise transport structure of the nonlinearity; it is the step that fails for the averaged equation (T1 below).

(3) *Viscous term.* `∫ |u| u_i Δu_i = −∫ ∂_j(|u| u_i) ∂_j u_i = −∫ |u| |∇u|^2 − ∫ u_i (∂_j |u|) ∂_j u_i = −∫ |u| |∇u|^2 − ∫ |u| |∇|u||^2`, by (1).

(4) *Pressure term.* `−∫ |u| u·∇p = ∫ p div(|u| u) = ∫ p (|u| div u + u·∇|u|) = ∫ p (u·∇|u|)`.

Multiplying `∂_t u = νΔu − (u·∇)u − ∇p` by `3|u| u` and integrating gives the claim. The `|u|^{3/2}` form is `∫ |∇w|^2 = (9/4) ∫ |u| |∇|u||^2`, so `3ν ∫ |u||∇|u||^2 = (4ν/3) ∫ |∇w|^2`. ∎

The identity is verified independently in Part 2 of the script (spectral, on `T^3`, where every step of the proof is the same): the direct `3 ∫ |u| u·∂_t u` and `−νD + P` agree, and the residual falls with the grid as the quadrature of the non-band-limited `|u|` improves. See D4.

### D3. Why the argument cannot close for large data — **PROVED-HERE**

Define, for smooth divergence-free `u ≢ 0` on `R^3` with `u` in the class (S) of the Setting (Schwartz `u` are the model case),

```text
K(u) := P(u) / ( X(u)^2 ‖u‖_{L^3} ),      K* := sup_u K(u),
Θ(u) := ν D(u) ‖u‖_{L^3} / P(u)   (when P(u) > 0),      κ_sharp := inf_u Θ(u).

Everything computed in D4 is a `T^3` number. **Proposition D3.** (a) `K` and `Θ` are invariant both under the Navier-Stokes scaling `u ↦ λ u(λ ·)` and under the amplitude scaling `u ↦ a u`, `a > 0`. (b) Step (iii) of D2 says exactly `K* ≤ θ = 2 C_p C_GN^{4/3}`; D2a gives the a priori bound, and D2b (KNOWN-conditional) global regularity, precisely on the scale-invariant set `{‖u^0‖_3 < (8ν/3)/θ}`. *Proof.* (a) From the scalings recorded in "Setting and notation": under `u ↦ u_λ` all of `Y`, `‖u‖_3` are invariant while `X^2`, `D`, `P` all carry the same factor `λ^2`, so the ratios are invariant; under `u ↦ au` we have `D(au) = a^3 D(u)`, `X(au)^2 = a^3 X(u)^2`, `‖au‖_3 = a ‖u‖_3` and `P(au) = a^4 P(u)`, so `K(au) = a^4/(a^3 · a) · K(u) = K(u)`, and `Θ(au) = a^3 · a/a^4 · Θ(u) = Θ(u)`. ### D3b. A class where the pressure work vanishes at any amplitude — **PROVED-HERE** and **COMPUTED**

**Proposition D3b.** Let `u` be smooth and divergence-free on `T^3 = (R/2πZ)^3` and *antiperiodic*: there is `a ∈ R^3` with `u(x + a) = −u(x)` for all `x`. Then `P(u) = 0`, so `d/dt ‖u‖_{L^3}^3 = −ν D(u) ≤ 0` at that instant, **for every amplitude**. In particular this holds for every divergence-free field whose Fourier support lies in the odd sublattice `{k : k_1 + k_2 + k_3 odd}` — e.g. the whole shell `|k| = 1` (the twelve real modes `cos x_j`, `sin x_j` with polarizations orthogonal to `e_j`), which contains the ABC flows.

*Proof.* Let `τ` denote translation by `a`. From `u ∘ τ = −u` we get `|u| ∘ τ = |u|` and, since `p = R_iR_j(u_iu_j)` is quadratic in `u` and translation-invariant, `p ∘ τ = p`. Hence `(u·∇|u|) ∘ τ = −(u·∇|u|)`, so `f := p (u·∇|u|)` satisfies `f ∘ τ = −f`. Translation preserves Lebesgue measure on `T^3`, so `∫ f = ∫ f ∘ τ = −∫ f`, i.e. `∫ f = 0` and `P(u) = 3∫f = 0`. For a field supported in the odd sublattice take `a = π(1,1,1)`: `e^{i k·a} = e^{iπ(k_1+k_2+k_3)} = −1` on that sublattice. ∎

**What this does and does not say.** It says the threshold of D2 is very far from being pointwise sharp: there are fields of arbitrarily large `L^3` norm at which `‖u‖_{L^3}` is instantaneously decreasing. It does **not** give any regularity, because the antiperiodicity class is not invariant under the flow: the Navier-Stokes nonlinearity is quadratic, hence *even* under `u ↦ −u`, while `∂_t` and `Δ` are odd, so `u ↦ −u(· + a)` is not a symmetry of the equation and the parity is destroyed immediately. This is the same phenomenon Line A records in a different guise (a functional can decrease at a state without being a Lyapunov functional). It is also the reason the numerical search of D4 finds nothing on the `|k| = 1` shell and must mix `|k|^2 = 1` with `|k|^2 = 2` to see the pressure work at all; D3c below gives the Fourier-side explanation (the leading term of `P` is a triad sum, and a single shell carries no triad) and turns it into a proof that `P ≢ 0`.

### D3c. The pressure work is not identically zero — **PROVED-HERE** on `T^3`, **CONJECTURE** on `R^3`

This section replaces a sentence that round 1 of refereeing found to be unsupported (`κ_sharp < ∞` because "D3b exhibits fields with `P(u) > 0`", which D3b does not do). The finiteness of the ceiling is settled here on the torus, by a proof, with an exactly evaluated constant.

**Proposition D3c(i) — the oddness dichotomy, PROVED-HERE, on `R^3` and on `T^3` alike.** The admissible class is closed under `u ↦ −u`, and `P(−u) = −P(u)` while `D(−u) = D(u)` (the pressure is quadratic, hence even; `u·∇|u|` is odd). Hence exactly one of the following holds: either `P ≡ 0` on the entire class — in which case `d/dt‖u‖_{L^3}^3 = −νD(u) ≤ 0` at *every* admissible field and `κ_sharp = +∞` — or there is an admissible `u` with `P(u) ≠ 0`, and then (replacing `u` by `−u` if needed) one with `P(u) > 0`, whence `κ_sharp ≤ Θ(u) < ∞`. **So proving `κ_sharp < ∞` is exactly proving `P ≢ 0`.** No optimization and no bound on `K*` is involved; the numerical search of D4 is not what settles this.

**Proposition D3c(ii) — `P ≢ 0` on `T^3`, PROVED-HERE.** Put `e_a = (0,1,0)`, `e_b = (1,0,0)`, `e_c = (1,−1,0)` and `k_a = (1,0,0)`, `k_b = (0,1,0)`, `k_c = (1,1,0)`; each `e` is orthogonal to its `k`, and `k_a + k_b = k_c` is a **triad**. For real `A, B, C` set

```text
v(x) = A e_a cos(x_1) + B e_b cos(x_2) + C e_c sin(x_1 + x_2)
     = ( B cos x_2 + C sin(x_1+x_2),  A cos x_1 − C sin(x_1+x_2),  0 ),
```

which is smooth and divergence-free on `T^3`. Fix `c > 0` and let `u_ε := c e_1 + ε v`. Then for `|ε| < c/‖v‖_∞`,

```text
P(u_ε) = 3 c ε^3 J(v) + O(ε^4),     J(v) := ∫_{T^3} q ∂_1 v_1 dx,   −Δq = ∂_i∂_j(v_i v_j),  ∫_{T^3} q = 0,
J(v) = −2 π^3 A B C   exactly.
```

With `A = B = C = 1`, `J = −2π^3 = −62.0125533606…`, so `P(u_ε) ≠ 0` for every sufficiently small `ε ≠ 0`, and `P(u_ε) > 0` for small `ε < 0`. Consequently `K*_{T^3} > 0` and `κ_sharp^{T^3} < ∞`.

*Proof.* (1) *The `O(ε)` pressure term vanishes because `v` is solenoidal.* `u_i u_j = c^2 δ_{i1}δ_{j1} + cε(δ_{i1}v_j + δ_{j1}v_i) + ε^2 v_i v_j`. The constant term has its only Fourier mode at `k = 0`, where the symbol of `R_iR_j` is set to zero (this is the zero-mean normalization of `p`), so it contributes nothing. The `O(ε)` term contributes `2c R_1R_j v_j`, whose symbol at `k` is `−2c k_1 (k·v̂(k))/|k|^2 = 0` because `k·v̂(k) = 0`. Hence, **exactly**, `p = ε^2 q` with `q := R_iR_j(v_iv_j)`.

(2) *Expansion of `|u_ε|`.* `|u_ε|^2 = c^2 + 2cεv_1 + ε^2|v|^2 ≥ (c − |ε|‖v‖_∞)^2 > 0` for `|ε| < c/‖v‖_∞`. So `ε ↦ |u_ε|(x)` is the composition of a polynomial in `ε` taking values in a fixed compact subinterval of `(0,∞)`, uniformly in `x`, with the analytic function `√·`; it is therefore real-analytic in `ε`, uniformly in `x`, and may be differentiated term by term in `x` as well. Explicitly `|u_ε| = c + εv_1 + ε^2(|v|^2 − v_1^2)/(2c) + O(ε^3)`, so `∇|u_ε| = ε∇v_1 + O(ε^2)` and `u_ε·∇|u_ε| = cε ∂_1v_1 + O(ε^2)`, with all remainders uniform in `x`.

(3) *The expansion of `P`.* `P(u_ε) = 3∫ p (u_ε·∇|u_ε|) = 3∫ ε^2 q (cε ∂_1v_1 + O(ε^2)) = 3cε^3 J(v) + O(ε^4)`, the integrals being over the compact `T^3` with uniformly bounded integrands. Thus `ε ↦ P(u_ε)` is real-analytic on `(−c/‖v‖_∞, c/‖v‖_∞)` with vanishing Taylor coefficients of orders `0,1,2` and `ε^3`-coefficient `3cJ(v)`; if `J(v) ≠ 0`, then `P(u_ε) ≠ 0` for all small `ε ≠ 0` and `sign P(u_ε) = sign(ε^3 J(v))`.

(4) *Exact evaluation of `J(v)`.* Since `∂_1v_1 = C cos(x_1+x_2)`, only the `(1,1,0)` Fourier component of `q` contributes. Neither `v_1^2` nor `v_2^2` has a `(1,1,0)` component: their cross terms are `2BC cos x_2 sin(x_1+x_2) = BC[sin(x_1+2x_2) + sin x_1]` and `−2AC cos x_1 sin(x_1+x_2) = −AC[sin(2x_1+x_2) + sin x_2]`, of frequencies `(1,2,0),(1,0,0),(2,1,0),(0,1,0)`, and their square terms have frequencies `(0,0,0)` and `(0,±2,0)`, `(±2,0,0)`, `(±2,±2,0)`. In `v_1v_2` the only `(1,1,0)` contribution is `AB cos x_1 cos x_2 = (AB/2)[cos(x_1+x_2) + cos(x_1−x_2)]`, i.e. `(AB/2)cos(x_1+x_2)`. Now `∂_i∂_j(v_iv_j) = ∂_1^2(v_1^2) + 2∂_1∂_2(v_1v_2) + ∂_2^2(v_2^2)` (the field is `x_3`-independent with `v_3 = 0`) and `∂_1∂_2 cos(x_1+x_2) = −cos(x_1+x_2)`, so the `(1,1,0)` part of `−Δq` is `2·(AB/2)·(−cos(x_1+x_2)) = −AB cos(x_1+x_2)`. Dividing by `|k|^2 = 2` gives that part of `q` as `−(AB/2)cos(x_1+x_2)`. Hence `J(v) = −(ABC/2)∫_{T^3}cos^2(x_1+x_2)dx = −(ABC/2)·4π^3 = −2π^3 ABC`. ∎

Part 5 of the script redoes step (4) independently and **symbolically**, from the Fourier coefficients of `v` and `J = (2π)^3 Σ_k q̂(k) conj(i k_1 v̂_1(k))` in exact `sympy` arithmetic, and returns `-2*pi**3*A*B*C`; it also verifies the expansion numerically (D4f). Two structural remarks. **Why a triad is needed**: `J` is trilinear in `v̂` and its terms are indexed by `k' + k'' + k''' = 0` with all three in the Fourier support of `v`, so a field supported on the single shell `|k| = 1` has `J = 0` — consistent with D3b, which gives the stronger exact statement `P = 0` there at every amplitude. **Why a mean flow is needed**: with `c = 0` the leading term is `O(ε^4)` and the argument gives nothing; the constant mode is what converts a cubic Fourier quantity into the leading behaviour of `P`. This is also the structural explanation of the D4(b) table, where the block with a mean `(3,0,0)` added is the one that behaves differently.

**D3c(iii) — the `R^3` statement is a CONJECTURE.** On `R^3` the construction above is unavailable: `c e_1` is not admissible, and it cannot be replaced by a nonconstant admissible `u_0`, because step (1) then fails — the `O(ε)` pressure term is `R_iR_j(u_{0i}v_j + u_{0j}v_i)`, whose symbol is `−k_ik_j/|k|^2` applied to the transform of `u_{0i}v_j`, and `div u_0 = div v = 0` no longer annihilates it mode by mode (one computes `∂_i∂_j(u_{0i}v_j) = (∂_i v_k)(∂_k u_{0i})`, which is not zero). **I therefore do not claim `P ≢ 0` on `R^3`, and by D3c(i) I do not claim `κ_sharp < ∞`.** The precise statement that would settle it, which I conjecture and do not prove: let `v` be periodic, smooth, divergence-free with `P_{T^3}(v) > 0`, let `ψ` be a periodic vector potential of `v`, let `χ ∈ C_c^∞(R^3)`, and set `u^{(L)} := curl(χ(·/L)ψ)`, a compactly supported divergence-free field on `R^3` equal to `χ(·/L)v + O(1/L)`. **CONJECTURE:** `P_{R^3}(u^{(L)}) = L^3 (2π)^{-3} (∫_{R^3}χ^4) P_{T^3}(v) + o(L^3)` as `L → ∞`. If that holds for one such `v` and `χ`, then `P_{R^3}(u^{(L)}) > 0` for large `L` and `κ_sharp < ∞`. The obstruction to proving it is exactly the nonlocality of the whole-space pressure: `p_{R^3}(χ(·/L)v)` is not `χ(·/L)^2 p_{T^3}(v)`, and the difference has to be shown to be `o(1)` in the norms that enter the quartic integrand — including near the edge of `supp χ`, where the sign of the local contribution is not controlled. Note also that even if the conjecture holds it gives **no number**: `Θ(u^{(L)}) ≈ Θ_{T^3}(v)·(L/2π) → ∞`, so this route bounds `κ_sharp` by nothing better than "finite".

### D5. The Type I reduction to a Liouville problem — **KNOWN**

Say a solution has a **Type I** singularity at `(x_0, T*)` if `|u(x,t)| ≤ C (T* − t)^{-1/2}` near it, and **Type II** otherwise. Leray's lower bound `‖u(t)‖_∞ ≥ c(T*−t)^{-1/2}` (`00_problem.md` item 2.5) says Type I is the *slowest possible* rate, so the dichotomy is exhaustive: a blowup is Type I (rate exactly `(T*−t)^{-1/2}`) or Type II (strictly faster).

**Reduction (KNOWN as a whole; the rescaling is corrected here).** Suppose `u` has a Type I singularity at `(x_0,T*)`, so `|u(x,t)| ≤ C(T*−t)^{-1/2}` near it. Take `t_k ↑ T*`, set `λ_k := (T* − t_k)^{1/2} ↓ 0`, and **centre the rescaling at `t_k`, not at `T*`**:

```text
U_k(y,s) := λ_k u(x_0 + λ_k y, t_k + λ_k^2 s),     s ∈ (−t_k/λ_k^2, 0].
```

Each `U_k` solves the same equations with the same `ν`. Since `T* − (t_k + λ_k^2 s) = λ_k^2(1 − s)`, the Type I bound gives, for `s ≤ 0`,

```text
|U_k(y,s)| ≤ λ_k · C (λ_k^2 (1−s))^{-1/2} = C (1 − s)^{-1/2} ≤ C,
```

**uniformly in `k` and on the whole of `s ≤ 0`** — this is the point of the recentring. Interior estimates for Navier-Stokes (Serrin/CKN local regularity from an `L^∞` bound) then give uniform bounds on all derivatives on compact subsets, and a diagonal extraction produces a limit `U` which is a *bounded* mild ancient solution on `R^3 × (−∞,0)`, with `sup_{s<0}‖U(s)‖_{L^∞} ≤ C`.

**What was wrong before, and why it mattered.** The previous version centred at `T*`, `U_k(y,s) := λ_k u(x_0 + λ_k y, T* + λ_k^2 s)`, for which the Type I bound gives only `|U_k(y,s)| ≤ λ_k C(T* − (T* + λ_k^2 s))^{-1/2} = C|s|^{-1/2}`, which **diverges as `s ↑ 0`**; the text then conceded uniform boundedness only on `{|s| ≥ ε}` and two clauses later announced a bounded ancient solution. That is not a wording slip: `A := sup_{s<0}‖U(s)‖_{L^∞} < ∞` is a hypothesis of both D6 and D7 and is the defining property of the KNSS class, so as written this file connected a Type I singularity to nothing at all. With the recentring above it does. An `ε`-regularity argument (`00_problem.md` item 2.6) shows `U ≢ 0` if `(x_0,T*)` was genuinely singular. Hence: **a Liouville theorem for bounded mild ancient solutions excludes Type I blowup.** **Citation dependence, at the point of use.** Three ingredients above are cited and unread: the interior estimates (Serrin/CKN), the compactness needed for the diagonal extraction, and the `ε`-regularity statement that gives `U ≢ 0`. The recentred bound `|U_k| ≤ C(1−s)^{-1/2}` is proved here in one line; everything after it is not. **The status of the reduction is therefore KNOWN, resting on papers whose hypotheses were never checked**, and a reader must verify in particular that the limit is *mild* (rather than merely a distributional solution) in the sense the Setting defines, since that is what D6 and D7 consume. ### D6. Liouville for bounded mild ancient solutions small in `L^∞_s L^3_x` — **PROVED-HERE**

Hence `c` is bounded and continuous, which is all that is used below.

(2) *Galilean normalization.* Set `h(s) := ∫_{−1}^{s} c(τ)dτ`, which is `C^1` with `h' = c` by step (1), and `Ũ(x,s) := U(x + h(s), s) − c(s) = V(x + h(s), s)`. Then `div Ũ = 0`, `‖Ũ(s)‖_{L^3} ≤ C_{BS} M`, `‖Ũ‖_∞ ≤ 2A + sup_s‖V(s)‖_∞ < ∞`, and `∇Ũ(x,s) = ∇U(x+h(s),s)` is bounded by (iii). **No pressure identity is asserted here** — the previous version wrote `p̃ = p(·+h) + c'(s)·x`, which presupposes that `c'` exists; step (3) below derives the existence of `c'` instead of assuming it.

(3) *The Duhamel representation, and `c' = −g` as a conclusion rather than a hypothesis.* Write `∇p = g(s) + ρ(·,s)`, so `sup_s‖ρ(s)‖_{L^{3/2}+L^3} = R < ∞` by (ii) and `ρ` is bounded by (ii)+(iii). Put `W(x,τ) := U(x + h(τ), τ) = Ũ(x,τ) + c(τ)`, which is bounded, smooth in `x`, and continuous in `τ` uniformly in `x` (by (iii) and `h ∈ C^1`). Fix `x ∈ R^3`, `s_0 < s < 0` and `η ∈ (0, s−s_0)`, and let `G_t` be the heat kernel. The map

```text
τ  ↦  (G_{s−τ} * W(·,τ))(x)      on [s_0, s−η]
```

is `C^1`, because `W`, `∇W`, `∇^2 W` and `∂_τ W` are bounded uniformly on `[s_0,s]` and `s−τ ≥ η > 0`; differentiating under the convolution and using `∂_t G_t = ΔG_t`, then moving the Laplacian onto `W` (legitimate since `ΔW` is bounded and continuous),

```text
d/dτ (G_{s−τ} * W(τ))(x) = G_{s−τ} * ( −ΔW(τ) + ∂_τ W(τ) )(x).
```

Now `∂_τ W(x,τ) = (∂_s U)(x+h,τ) + (c(τ)·∇U)(x+h,τ)` and, by the equation, `∂_s U = ΔU − (U·∇)U − ∇p`. Since `∇U(x+h,τ) = ∇Ũ(x,τ)` and `U(x+h,τ) − c(τ) = Ũ(x,τ)`, the two Laplacians cancel and the transport terms combine to `−(Ũ·∇)Ũ(x,τ) = −div(Ũ⊗Ũ)(x,τ)` (using `div Ũ = 0`). Hence

```text
d/dτ (G_{s−τ} * W(τ))(x) = − G_{s−τ} * [ div(Ũ⊗Ũ)(τ) + ρ(· + h(τ), τ) ](x) − g(τ),
```

the last term because `G_t` has unit mass and so fixes constants. Every function convolved here is bounded and continuous, so the right-hand side is continuous in `τ` on `[s_0,s−η]`. Integrating from `s_0` to `s−η` and letting `η ↓ 0` (using `G_η * W(s−η) → W(s)` pointwise, by the uniform continuity of `W` in `τ` and boundedness) gives, for every `x`,

```text
Ũ(s) + c(s) = e^{(s−s_0)Δ}Ũ(s_0) + c(s_0) − ∫_{s_0}^{s} e^{(s−τ)Δ}[ div(Ũ⊗Ũ) + ρ(· + h(τ), τ) ] dτ − ∫_{s_0}^{s} g(τ) dτ.
```

**This replaces the appeal to Tychonov uniqueness, which was a non sequitur.** The old text justified the formula by "the difference solves the heat equation with zero data and lies in `L^∞ + L^{3/2} + L^3`, hence has at most Gaussian growth". Membership in `L^{3/2} + L^3` implies no pointwise bound at all, let alone `|v(x,t)| ≤ Ce^{a|x|^2}`, and the one term whose pointwise size was genuinely unclear was the `ρ`-Duhamel term (the naive `‖e^{tΔ}ρ_1‖_∞ ≤ ct^{-1}‖ρ_1‖_{3/2}` is not integrable in `τ`). The argument above never leaves the pointwise world: it is the fundamental theorem of calculus applied to a `C^1` function of `τ`, and it uses `ρ` bounded — which (ii) and (iii) supply — rather than any growth class.

Now read off the constant. Every term except `c(s) − c(s_0) + ∫_{s_0}^s g` lies in `L^3 + L^{3/2}(R^3)`: `Ũ(s) ∈ L^3`; `e^{tΔ}Ũ(s_0) ∈ L^3`; `∫‖e^{(s−τ)Δ}div(Ũ⊗Ũ)‖_{L^3}dτ ≤ c∫_{s_0}^s (s−τ)^{-1/2}‖Ũ⊗Ũ‖_{L^3}dτ ≤ 2c(s−s_0)^{1/2}‖Ũ‖_∞ C_{BS}M < ∞`; and `∫‖e^{(s−τ)Δ}ρ(·+h,τ)‖_{L^{3/2}+L^3}dτ ≤ (s−s_0)R < ∞` by (ii), the heat semigroup being a contraction on each `L^q`. But `L^3 + L^{3/2}(R^3)` contains no nonzero constant (if `e ≠ 0` were one, `|e| R^3 ≲ ‖·‖` over `B_R` would force `|e|R^3 = O(R^2)`). Hence `c(s) − c(s_0) = −∫_{s_0}^s g(τ)dτ` for all `s_0 < s`: **`c` is absolutely continuous with `c' = −g` a.e., proved rather than assumed**, and this also removes the previous version's unjustified appeal to the smoothness of `c`. Setting `p̃(x,s) := p(x + h(s), s) − g(s)·x` — now legitimate, since `c'(s) = −g(s)` — we get `∇p̃ = ρ(· + h(s), s) ∈ L^{3/2}+L^3`, i.e. (ii) with `g = 0`, and the display above becomes the Duhamel formula used in step (4).

(4) *`Ũ` is mild.* Let `q := R_iR_j(Ũ_iŨ_j)`; then `div(Ũ⊗Ũ) + ∇q = P div(Ũ⊗Ũ)` and `Δ(p̃ − q) = 0`. Moreover `∇q ∈ L^3`, since `∂_k(Ũ_iŨ_j) ∈ L^3` (as `Ũ ∈ L^3` and `∇Ũ ∈ L^∞`) and `R_iR_j` is bounded on `L^3`. So by step (3), `∇(p̃−q)` is a harmonic vector field in `L^{3/2}+L^3`; writing it as `g_1 + g_2` and using the mean value property, `|∇(p̃−q)(x)| = |avg_{B_R(x)}| ≤ cR^{-2}‖g_1‖_{3/2} + cR^{-1}‖g_2‖_3 → 0` as `R → ∞`, so `∇(p̃−q) ≡ 0`. Substituting into the Duhamel formula of (3) turns it into the mild formulation for `Ũ`, and the linear term tends to `0` in `L^∞` as `s_0 → −∞` as in D6.

(5) *Conclusion.* `Ũ` is a bounded mild ancient solution with `sup_s ‖Ũ(s)‖_{L^3} ≤ C_{BS} M < ε_0`, so D6 gives `Ũ ≡ 0`, i.e. `V ≡ 0`, `Ω = curl V ≡ 0`, and `U(x,s) = c(s) =: b(s)` with `b' = −g` a.e. by step (3). Finally `U` is itself a bounded mild ancient solution and is spatially constant, so `U ⊗ U` is constant, `P div(U⊗U) = 0`, `e^{tΔ}b(s_0) = b(s_0)`, and the mild formula gives `b(s) = b(s_0)` for every `s_0 < s`. Hence `b` is a constant vector and `g = −b' = 0` a.e. ∎

**Honest placement.** D7 is *strictly weaker than ESS* as a blowup exclusion: `‖u‖_{L^3} ≤ C_{BS} ‖ω‖_{L^{3/2}}` (modulo constants) by the same Biot-Savart estimate, so a solution with `sup_t ‖ω(t)‖_{L^{3/2}} < ∞` already has `sup_t ‖u(t)‖_{L^3} < ∞` and is regular by ESS (item 2.4) — no smallness needed and no Type I hypothesis needed. What D7 adds is: (a) a complete, elementary proof (heat semigroup, Hölder, Liouville for harmonic functions — no Carleman inequality, no backward uniqueness, no partial regularity), (b) the identification of the exact obstruction to removing the smallness, and (c) an argument whose engine (D6) plausibly transfers to the averaged equation, which ESS does not — though that transfer is itself only a CONJECTURE, see the T1 row. Hypothesis (ii) is a normalization of the pressure at spatial infinity, of exactly the kind `00_problem.md` §1.2 and §1.5 flag as part of the specification; whether the ancient solutions produced by the D5 rescaling satisfy (ii) in this quantitative form is **not verified here** and is recorded in the Breakpoint. Hypothesis (iii) is the regularity of the mild class, KNOWN [unverified] and carried as a hypothesis precisely because it is unread; a reader who does not accept it should read D7 as conditional on it too.

### D8. What "smallness" can mean for ancient solutions; and a FAILED attempt

**(a) Scaling triviality of non-invariant smallness — PROVED-HERE.** The class `A` of bounded mild ancient solutions is invariant under `S_λ : U ↦ λ U(λ ·, λ^2 ·)` for every `λ > 0`, and `S_λ` is a bijection of `A` with inverse `S_{1/λ}`. Let `q : A → [0,∞]` be any functional with `q(S_λ U) = λ^a q(U)` for some `a > 0`. Then for every `ε > 0` the statement "`U ∈ A` and `q(U) < ε` imply `U` trivial" is **equivalent** to the unrestricted Liouville statement for `A`: given any `U ∈ A` with `q(U) < ∞`, choose `λ` with `λ^a q(U) < ε`, apply the hypothesis to `S_λ U`, and pull back. *Consequences:* (i) `q = ‖U‖_{L^∞}` has `a = 1`, so **no `L^∞`-smallness hypothesis carries any information**; (ii) `q = ` the Lipschitz constant of the vorticity direction `ξ = Ω/|Ω|` in `x` has `a = 1` (since `ξ_λ(y,s) = ξ(λy, λ^2 s)`), so in any Liouville theorem under a Constantin-Fefferman-type hypothesis **the Lipschitz constant may be assumed as small as one likes** — the content of the hypothesis is the qualitative statement that `ξ` is Lipschitz at all, not the size of the constant. By contrast `sup_s‖U(s)‖_{L^3}` (D6), `sup_s‖Ω(s)‖_{L^{3/2}}` (D7), and `sup_{r,z} r^{-2}∬_{Q_r(z)}(|U|^3+|P|^{3/2})` are scale-invariant (`a = 0`), so smallness in them is a genuine restriction.

**(b) FAILED: a Liouville theorem under a Lipschitz vorticity direction.** *Attempt.* Let `U ∈ A` with `ξ = Ω/|Ω|` Lipschitz in `x` uniformly in `s` where `Ω ≠ 0`; by (a)(ii) assume the constant `L` is as small as desired. Constantin-Fefferman's geometric depletion (`00_problem.md` item 2.7 refinements [unverified]) bounds the vortex-stretching factor `α = ξ·(∇U)ξ` by a Biot-Savart integral whose kernel carries a factor `|sin θ(x,y)|` measuring the misalignment of `ξ(x)` and `ξ(y)`; with `ξ` Lipschitz, `|sinθ| ≤ L|x−y|`, so `|α(x)| ≲ L ∫ |x−y|^{-2} |Ω(y)| dy = L · I_1(|Ω|)(x)`. Feeding this into `d/ds ∫ |Ω|^2 = 2∫ α |Ω|^2 − 2∫|∇Ω|^2` and using Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev `‖I_1 f‖_{L^3} ≤ c‖f‖_{L^{3/2}}` gives `∫ α|Ω|^2 ≤ ‖I_1|Ω|‖_3 ‖Ω‖_{L^3}^2 ≲ L ‖Ω‖_{3/2} ‖Ω‖_3^2`, and one would then absorb `‖Ω‖_3^2` into the dissipation and take `L` small.

*Where it breaks — the exact step.* The very first display after the depletion bound. `∫ α |Ω|^2 ≤ ‖I_1|Ω|‖_{L^3}‖Ω‖_{L^3}^2` requires `Ω(·,s) ∈ L^{3/2} ∩ L^3`, and a bounded ancient solution has **no decay at all** — `Ω` is merely in `L^∞`, and `∫|Ω|^2` is typically infinite, so the global enstrophy inequality that the argument differentiates does not exist. The natural repair is to localize: replace `∫|Ω|^2` by `∫ χ_R^2 |Ω|^2` with `χ_R(x) = χ(x/R)`. This fails for a reason that is structural, not technical: every term in the localized inequality is scale-invariant in the same way. The commutator terms produced by the cutoff, `∫ |Ω|^2 (U·∇)χ_R^2` and `∫ |Ω|^2 |∇χ_R|^2`, carry `R^{-1}` and `R^{-2}` against a main term that also carries `R^{-1}`-type weights after the ancient-solution rescaling `S_R`, so passing `R → ∞` does not make them subordinate; and the Riesz potential `I_1(χ_R|Ω|)` differs from `χ_R I_1(|Ω|)` by a tail that is of the same order as the main term because `|Ω|` does not decay. In short: the Constantin-Fefferman method is a *finite-energy* method, and the ancient-solution class is exactly the class where finite energy has been scaled away. Status **FAILED**; no partial result is claimed. (For the record, D8(a)(ii) is not affected: it is a statement about hypotheses, not about this proof.)

### D9. Is Tao's averaged blowup Type I or Type II? — **CONJECTURE**

Tao's averaged equation (`00_problem.md` item 2.2) obeys the same energy identity and the same scaling. Leray's lower bound argument uses only local well-posedness in a subcritical norm plus scaling, and I *expect* it to transfer, giving `‖u(t)‖_{L^∞} ≥ c(T*−t)^{-1/2}` — the blowup would then be **at least** Type I in rate. **CONJECTURE, not verified**: local well-posedness for `B̃` in a subcritical norm is a property of a construction whose text was never read (only the abstract of Tao 2016 was seen; `00_problem.md` item 2.2), and the previous version asserted "transfers verbatim" flatly. Everything in this subsection is downstream of that assumption. Which of the two it is depends on how much energy the cascade keeps at the active scale, and I could not verify this against the paper in this environment. Two readings:

- *Energy-preserving cascade (my reading of the "self-replicating machine").* If a fixed fraction of the energy `E` sits at scale `L_n ~ 2^{-n}` at stage `n`, then the amplitude is `A_n ~ (E/L_n^3)^{1/2} ~ 2^{3n/2}`, the eddy-turnover time of stage `n` is `T_n ~ L_n/A_n ~ 2^{-5n/2}` (summable, hence finite-time blowup), and `T* − t ~ 2^{-5n/2}` gives `‖u(t)‖_∞ ~ A_n ~ (T*−t)^{-3/5}`. Since `3/5 > 1/2`, this is **Type II**.
- *Exactly discretely self-similar blowup.* Then `‖u(t)‖_∞ ~ (T*−t)^{-1/2}` by definition, i.e. **Type I**, and the energy in the singular region must vanish like `(T*−t)^{1/2}` — the cascade would be losing essentially all of its energy at each stage, which is not how a "self-replicating machine" is described.

I mark the `3/5` computation **CONJECTURE** [unverified: the exponent is reconstructed from the scaling of an energy-preserving cascade, not read off Tao's theorem]. The consequence, either way, is recorded in the transfer check: if Tao's blowup is Type II, then the D5 reduction has nothing to say about it, which is consistent — the Liouville route is a Type I route only.

## Breakpoint

This line stops in three distinct places; each is a precisely stated open step, not a technical gap.

**B1 (the `L^3` wall, D2/D3).** The estimate closes only for `‖u^0‖_{L^3} < κ_sharp`. On `T^3`, `κ_sharp^{T^3} < ∞` because `K*_{T^3} > 0`, which is now **proved** (D3c(ii): `P(c e_1 + εv) = 3cε^3 J(v) + O(ε^4)` with `J(v) = −2π^3 ABC ≠ 0`), not merely computed as the previous version claimed. On `R^3`, `κ_sharp < ∞` is **CONJECTURE** (D3c(iii)): by the oddness dichotomy D3c(i) it is equivalent to `P ≢ 0` on the Schwartz class, and no Schwartz field with `P ≠ 0` is exhibited anywhere in this line. The exact breaking step is step (iv) of D2: the bracket `θ‖u(t)‖_{L^3} − 8ν/3` multiplying `X^2`. Both terms are of the same homogeneity under both the parabolic scaling and the amplitude scaling (D3a), so the dissipation cannot dominate the pressure work at large data, at any scale, in any covering or induction. To go further one must show that the pressure work `P(u) = 3∫p(u·∇|u|)` is *systematically* smaller along solutions than its Hölder bound allows — i.e. use the dynamics, not a pointwise inequality on fields. Nothing in this line does that. **The quantitative distance to the truth is unknown, and more completely unknown than the previous version admitted.** There is no upper bound of any kind on the `R^3` quantity `κ_sharp` in this file: the number `152ν` is `Θ` at a `T^3` trigonometric polynomial, which is not an admissible competitor and is not a limit of admissible competitors (D3, D4d). What is bracketed is `κ_sharp^{T^3} ≤ 152ν`, a torus quantity, against `κ_sharp ≥ 0.167ν`, a whole-space quantity: two bounds on two different functionals. **This check — truncate the periodic optimizer at scale `L` and see whether the functional converges or diverges — should be applied to every claim in this project that computes on `T^3` and states on `R^3`.**

**B2 (the ancient Liouville problem, D6/D7).** Both theorems need smallness of a scale-invariant quantity, and there is no mechanism here to remove it. The obstruction is visible in the proof of D6: the fixed-point inequality `A ≤ 8(C_2C_6)^{1/2} N A` is a *perturbative* statement — it is the ancient-solution form of Kato's small-data theory, and it uses nothing but bilinear estimates and the heat semigroup. Removing the smallness is, by item 2.4, essentially the content of ESS (blowup/rescaling plus backward uniqueness via Carleman inequalities). It probably *cannot* be done with the tools used here, because the same tools plausibly apply to the averaged equation, which has a nontrivial blowup — but that last step is the T1 conjecture about the internals of `B̃`, not a verified fact, so the obstruction is stated here as a strong expectation rather than as a theorem. A secondary, smaller gap: hypothesis (ii) of D7 (a pressure normalization at spatial infinity) is assumed, not verified for the ancient solutions produced by the D5 rescaling.

**B3 (the reduction itself, D5/D9).** Even a complete Liouville theorem for bounded mild ancient solutions would only exclude **Type I** blowup. Type II is untouched, and D9 suggests (CONJECTURE) that the one blowup we actually possess in a Navier-Stokes-like system — Tao's averaged equation — is Type II. So the Liouville route, even if it succeeded, would not close (A)/(B); it would reduce the problem to Type II blowup, about which this line says nothing.

## What would be needed next

- **Dynamical depletion of the pressure work.** The single most useful object would be a bound of the form `∫_0^T P(u(t)) dt ≤ (1−η) (8ν/3) ∫_0^T X(t)^2 dt + (controlled error)` valid along solutions with `‖u^0‖_3` large, i.e. a statement true on trajectories but false on fields (D4e shows the pointwise statement is false). By T1 the proof of any such bound must use the exact nonlinearity — the natural candidate being the transport structure that already made D1 step (2) vanish, applied to `p` itself through `Dp/Dt`.
- **The sharp constants.** `C_p` (the `L^3` norm of `F ↦ R_iR_jF_{ij}` on symmetric matrix fields; equivalently, after removing the trace, a Beurling-Ahlfors-type operator in 3D) is bracketed here only in `[0.398, 18.72]`. Determining it, or even improving the upper bound below `3`, would move `κ` by an order of magnitude; the traceless decomposition `p = −|u|^2/3 + R_iR_jD_{ij}` with `|D| = √(2/3)|u|^2` is the obvious starting point and was not pushed here.
- **A `T^3`-to-`R^3` transference, or a direct `R^3` computation.** This is now the most valuable missing item in the first half of the line, and it is the one the referee's truncation check exposes. Either prove the CONJECTURE of D3c(iii) — `P_{R^3}(curl(χ(·/L)ψ)) = L^3(2π)^{-3}(∫χ^4)P_{T^3}(v) + o(L^3)`, which would give `κ_sharp < ∞` on `R^3` — or compute `D`, `P` and `‖u‖_3` directly for a compactly supported divergence-free field on `R^3`, using a box several periods wide so that the periodic images contribute at the `O(L^{-3})` level, and watch the sign of `P` stabilize under box refinement. Until one of these is done, **this line provides no upper bound at all on the `R^3` quantity `κ_sharp`**, and the number `152` says nothing about it.
- **A better probe of `κ_sharp^{T^3}`.** The partial descent in D4d is over 52 low-frequency periodic modes and almost certainly misses the maximizer of `K` on `T^3`. A search over anisotropic, spatially concentrated profiles with an adjoint-based gradient would tighten the torus upper bound enormously; on `R^3` it would, together with the previous item, say whether `κ` is off by `10` or by `10^3`.
- **Hypotheses (ii) and (iii) of D7, and the smoothness of the mild class.** Show that the ancient solutions produced by the D5 rescaling of a Type I blowup admit a pressure with `sup_s ‖∇p(s) − g(s)‖_{L^{3/2}+L^3} < ∞` for some bounded `g`, or restate D7 in the KNSS mild class without any pressure hypothesis. Hypothesis (iii) — a bounded mild ancient solution is smooth with `∇U`, `∇^2U`, `∂_sU` bounded — should be *proved* here rather than cited: it is a parabolic-regularity statement about the mild formulation and is very likely within reach of the same Oseen estimates that D6 uses, which would remove one unread citation from a load-bearing position.
- **Whether D6's hypothesis is ever satisfiable at a blowup.** Recorded in D6 remark (3): by ESS the rescaled family has `‖U_k(s)‖_{L^3} → ∞`, Fatou gives the inequality in the unhelpful direction, and nothing here shows that the D5 limit can have `sup_s ‖U(s)‖_{L^3} < ε_0`. If it never can, D6 excludes nothing and the Type I programme rests on D7 alone.
- **Type II.** By B3 the Liouville programme is a Type I programme. The corresponding reduction for Type II (which would produce a limit that is not bounded, and thus is not in the KNSS class at all) does not exist and is where the actual difficulty of (A)/(B) lives.
- **Settle D9** by reading Tao 2016 §1 for the blowup rate; a one-line check of whether `‖u(t)‖_{L^∞} (T*−t)^{1/2}` is bounded in his construction decides whether the Type I reduction is even relevant to the one blowup that is known.
